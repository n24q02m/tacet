\begin{abstract}
Large language models make knowledge-graph question answering (KGQA)
fluent, but every query pays the full price of an LLM call. We present
\textbf{TACET} (Latin: \emph{it is silent}), a self-distilling
neuro-symbolic cascade that amortises that cost by progressively migrating
a streamed workload off the LLM and onto cheap, checkable tiers, so that
the expensive teacher falls silent over time. The cascade has three tiers:
a \emph{sound} forward-chaining Datalog rule engine that abstains when it
cannot prove an answer (Tier~1), a confidence-calibrated ComplEx link
predictor~\citep{trouillon2016complex,guo2017calibration} verified against
a typed ontology (Tier~2), and an LLM teacher (Tier~3). The central
mechanism is an \emph{online distillation} loop. Whenever the teacher
answers, TACET mines the answer into Datalog-checkable Horn rules and
writes the facts back, so routing drifts toward the cheap tiers as the
workload streams. A synthesised rule generalises to entities the teacher
never saw; an answer cache cannot do that.

On a controlled KGQA benchmark (\seeds{} seeds), the cascade answers at \akAcc{} accuracy while reducing
blended cost by ${\sim}\costFactor\times$ relative to an LLM-only system
under a \emph{simulated per-tier cost model}. We then confirm this on real
data with a real teacher. Streaming MetaQA (a $43$k-entity movie KG)
through a real Grok~4.3 teacher over \textbf{3 seeds} in a controlled
design (Tier-2 disabled and a single teacher answer shared across arms, so
accuracy is matched \emph{by construction}), TACET amortises
\emph{measured} LLM dollars by $2.8\times$ on 1-hop and $5.1\times$ on
2-hop in pooled cost; the per-seed cost ratio spans $1.5$--$6.3\times$ on
1-hop because only non-empty teacher answers are cached. This saving is
delivered by answer reuse, the cascade's caching tier. Under the real
teacher, rule distillation adds no dollar advantage: full distillation ties
the cache on every seed. The distillation-over-caching effect is itself
\emph{teacher-quality-gated}. With an oracle teacher the miner recovers a
generalising composition rule and makes $87\%$ fewer teacher calls than a
cache, because a rule answers unseen heads and a cache cannot; under the
noisy real LLM the miner recovers no installable rule and the cascade
reduces to a cache. Sweeping the installation threshold across eleven 2026
frontier teachers refines this: at the paper's operating point none installs
the rule, but at a lower threshold ten of eleven (favourable seed per model)
install the world-precision-$1.0$ composition rule at $81$--$86\%$ fewer calls, so the gate
is the confidence threshold, not teacher identity. That regime installs a
self-referential junk rule alongside the true one, which appears to demand gold
labels to tell apart; the natural label-free substitutes (teacher agreement,
self-consistency) are empirically inert. The apparent dependency is an artifact
of candidate enumeration: excluding the mining target from its own rule body
removes every junk rule while leaving the true rule and its savings untouched,
so what still limits the regime is seed-dependence and the near-functional
leakage of Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision}, not a need for gold
(Section~\ref{sec:threshold-gate}). The one remaining structural foreclosure
of that regime --- genuinely recursive targets, excluded by the same blunt
enumeration rule --- is itself removable: a refined guard that bans only the
pure self-loop body preserves the published result exactly on the real MetaQA
grid ($22/22$ cells, by classification and generative replay) and, on a
synthetic transitive-closure grid with oracle teachers, extends coverage to
genuinely recursive targets (Appendix~\ref{sec:recursive-boundary}).

Tier-1 answers carry replayable Datalog proof trees,
and we prove an ontology-preservation guarantee
(Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}) for the synthesised rules. The
distillation-over-caching effect that is neutral under the noisy KGQA
teacher turns \emph{positive} on a second domain: streaming GDPR
compliance cases from PrivaCI-Bench~\citep{privacibench2025} past three real
frontier teachers (gemini-3.5-flash, claude-sonnet-4.6, grok-4.3) over $3$
seeds at matched verdict accuracy, online rule distillation amortises measured
teacher cost by $1.5$--$2.1\times$ versus an LLM-only system --- against
${\sim}1.1\times$ for an exact-match cache and ${<}1\times$ for a faithful
in-context-strategy baseline --- with only a ${\sim}10\%$ repeat ceiling, so the
saving is rule \emph{generalisation}, not memoisation. We release the
implementation, the benchmark generator, and the full experiment grid.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models made knowledge-graph question answering (KGQA) and
retrieval-augmented generation fluent~\citep{lewis2020rag, edge2024graphrag},
but they made it \emph{uniformly expensive}: every query, however routine,
pays the full price of an LLM call. Much of a real KGQA workload is regular
--- a base-fact lookup, the transitive closure of a relation, a structurally
predictable edge --- and does not need a frontier model at all. The
opportunity is to recognise that regular majority cheaply, serve it without
the LLM, and reserve the LLM for the genuinely hard tail; the difficulty is
that a static front-end (a cache, a fixed cascade) cannot \emph{learn} which
queries are regular as a workload reveals itself.

Model cascades address part of this by routing inputs through tiers of
increasing cost and stopping early when a cheap tier is
confident~\citep{viswanathan2019cascade, chen2023frugalgpt}. But a static
cascade has a \emph{fixed} routing distribution: it never gets cheaper. A
semantic cache adapts to repeats, but a cache can only return an answer it
has \emph{literally seen} --- it cannot answer an unseen entity, and most
distinct queries in a streaming workload are unseen. The missing ingredient
is a mechanism that turns each expensive LLM answer into reusable,
\emph{generalising} structure, so that answering one query makes a whole
\emph{class} of related queries cheap thereafter.

\textbf{TACET} supplies that mechanism. The name --- Latin for \emph{it is
silent}, the musical direction for a voice that rests --- is the design goal:
as distilled rules accumulate, the expensive LLM teacher is called ever less
often, until on a recurring workload it falls silent altogether. It is a
self-distilling neuro-symbolic cascade: each LLM answer is mined into
Datalog-checkable Horn rules and written back as graph facts, so the routing
distribution drifts toward the cheap tiers as the workload streams. Because a synthesised rule
covers \emph{unseen} entities, distillation amortises an entire relational
pattern, not just a single memoised answer --- the property that separates it
from caching. The cheap tiers are also \emph{checkable}: Tier~1 answers carry
replayable Datalog proof trees that ground in base facts and named rules.
Concretely, \textbf{TACET} is a three-tier cascade:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier 1 --- Symbolic.} A forward-chaining rule engine over a
  typed property graph. It is \emph{sound}: any answer it returns is
  entailed by the facts and rules; when it cannot prove an answer it
  \emph{abstains}. Cost ${\sim}10^{-4}$ per query.
  \item \textbf{Tier 2 --- KGE.} A ComplEx~\citep{trouillon2016complex}
  link predictor that proposes a missing edge with a temperature-calibrated
  confidence~\citep{guo2017calibration}; predictions are verified against
  the ontology before use. Cost ${\sim}10^{-3}$.
  \item \textbf{Tier 3 --- LLM teacher.} A frontier LLM that answers
  anything. Cost ${\sim}5\times10^{-2}$.
\end{itemize}

The component we claim as novel is the \emph{conjunction}: an online
distillation loop whose artifact is \emph{executable} --- type-checked
Datalog rules and facts inside a sound symbolic tier --- rather than
retrieved text or student parameters; Section~\ref{sec:related} situates
this against cascades that learn over time. Whenever the
teacher answers, TACET (a) \emph{writes the fact back} into the graph, so
the identical query drops to Tier~1; (b) runs an AMIE-style rule
miner~\citep{galarraga2015amie} over the accumulated teacher answers and,
when a high-confidence Horn rule emerges, installs it in the sound Tier~1
--- so an entire \emph{relational pattern}, not just one fact, becomes
cheap and covers \emph{unseen} entities; and (c) augments the Tier-2
training set. Over a streamed workload the routing distribution drifts
downward and the blended cost decays toward the symbolic floor.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(C1)] \textbf{A self-distilling cascade.} An online loop that mines
  each LLM teacher's answers into Datalog-checkable Horn rules and writes the
  facts back, so the routing distribution drifts toward the cheap tiers as a
  workload streams. We isolate the \emph{distillation-beats-caching}
  mechanism: when the miner recovers a generalising rule, a synthesised rule
  covers entities the teacher never saw, so a whole relational pattern becomes
  cheap --- something a cache, which only recalls answers it has literally
  seen, structurally cannot do
  (Sections~\ref{sec:cascade}--\ref{sec:distill}). On real data we find this
  advantage is \emph{teacher-quality-gated} (C3): it materialises with an
  accurate teacher and degrades to cache-equivalent behaviour under a noisy
  one.
  \item[(C2)] \textbf{Auditability with a typed-closure guarantee.} Tier-1
  answers carry replayable Datalog proof trees that ground in base facts and
  named rules, and we prove an \emph{ontology-preservation} guarantee for the
  online-synthesised rules (Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}): the engine installs
  a rule only if it is type-consistent, and write-back types each new entity
  from the relation's declared schema, so the symbolic closure preserves the
  typed ontology whenever the written-back facts are themselves well-typed. A
  noisy teacher that asserts a type-inconsistent fact between existing entities
  can still introduce a violation; we surface such facts with an explicit
  validator rather than assume they cannot occur (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
  \item[(C3)] \textbf{A real-LLM, real-cost evaluation.} We evaluate the
  cascade with a real Grok~4.3 teacher on a real-entity MetaQA KG, metering
  every token at xAI's published price (Section~\ref{sec:realcost}).
  Over \textbf{3 seeds} in a controlled design (Tier-2 disabled, a single
  teacher answer shared across arms so accuracy is matched by construction),
  the measured cost amortisation is $2.8\times$
  (1-hop) and $5.1\times$ (2-hop) over LLM-only in pooled cost (per-seed ratios
  span $1.5$--$6.3\times$ on 1-hop) --- consistent
  with the simulated cost model. This saving is
  delivered by answer reuse, not rule distillation, which under the real
  teacher ties the cache on every seed. We
  further show the distillation-over-caching advantage is
  \emph{teacher-quality-gated}: positive with an oracle teacher ($87\%$ fewer
  teacher calls than a cache, recovering a generalising composition rule for
  unseen heads) but neutral under the noisy real LLM, which on this benchmark
  reduces the cascade to a cache. Concretely, the installation gate is the
  miner's confidence threshold $\gamma$, not teacher accuracy directly --- even a
  $0$--$5\%$-accurate teacher installs the rule at $\gamma{=}0.50$
  (Section~\ref{sec:threshold-gate}) --- and \emph{teacher-quality-gated} means
  only that at the paper's deployable operating point $\gamma{=}0.95$ teacher
  quality decides whether the rule clears the bar. We do not claim distillation
  beats caching with a real LLM here. A threshold sweep over eleven 2026 teachers further
  shows the MetaQA null is governed by the installation threshold rather than
  teacher identity (Section~\ref{sec:threshold-gate}). The low-threshold regime
  it exposes is bounded by three findings there: the obvious label-free
  promotion signals are inert, the cross-teacher ranking is confounded by answer
  verbosity, and the junk rule that appeared to force a gold dependency is an
  artifact of candidate enumeration that a one-line change removes at no cost to
  the true rule.
  \item[(C4)] \textbf{An honest negative result.} A candid study of graph
  \emph{forecasting} showing that a learned forward-dynamics extension does
  not beat purpose-built temporal models, reported as a negative result
  rather than a headline claim (Section~\ref{sec:layer4}).
  \item[(C5)] \textbf{A second domain where distillation beats caching with a
  real teacher.} We port the identical cascade to regulatory-compliance
  checking on PrivaCI-Bench~\citep{privacibench2025} (GDPR), mining online
  Datalog rules over typed contextual-integrity atoms from a real teacher's
  verdicts. Over $3$ seeds at matched verdict accuracy, full distillation
  amortises measured teacher cost by $1.5$--$2.1\times$ across three frontier
  teachers, against ${\sim}1.1\times$ for an exact-match cache and ${<}1\times$
  for a faithful in-context-strategy baseline (an Inter-Cascade~\citep{intercascade2025}
  port at the same model); with a ${\sim}10\%$ repeat ceiling the gain is rule
  generalisation, not memoisation (Section~\ref{sec:compliance}). This is the
  positive real-(non-oracle)-teacher answer to the question (ii) of
  Section~\ref{sec:realcost} that the KGQA workload leaves neutral.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} The main grid evaluates on a \emph{controlled synthetic}
benchmark with the LLM teacher modelled as an (optionally noisy) oracle and a
\emph{simulated} constant per-tier cost. This isolates the phenomenon we
study --- the dynamics of routing and distillation --- from the orthogonal
question of LLM answer quality. The real-LLM, real-data result that closes
this gap is reported in Section~\ref{sec:realcost}; the extension modules
shipped but not invoked by the cascade are described in
Appendix~\ref{sec:implementation}. We use MetaQA and ProofWriter as
\emph{mechanism testbeds}: both have long been saturated by specialised
systems~\citep{saxena2020embedkgqa,tafjord2021proofwriter}, so we make no
state-of-the-art accuracy claim on either; they are small enough to meter a
real teacher end-to-end, which is the measurement this paper is about.
The cascade is a domain-general mechanism, not a KGQA-specific system; to
show this we evaluate it on a second, non-saturated domain --- regulatory
\emph{compliance checking} on PrivaCI-Bench~\citep{privacibench2025} (GDPR) ---
where, unlike on the noisy KGQA teacher, rule distillation amortises a real
(non-oracle) teacher's cost beyond a cache across three frontier LLMs
(Section~\ref{sec:compliance}). MetaQA and ProofWriter remain mechanism
testbeds; compliance is the external-validity domain.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Model cascades and adaptive inference.} Cascades route inputs
through models of increasing cost, stopping early when a cheap model is
confident~\citep{viswanathan2019cascade}. FrugalGPT~\citep{chen2023frugalgpt}
cascades LLMs of different prices for general prompts. TACET differs in
two ways that matter. First, its tiers span \emph{different
representations} --- symbolic, embedding, neural --- not just different-sized
models of the same kind; its cheapest tier is therefore \emph{sound} rather
than merely cheap. Second, and centrally, a static cascade has a
\emph{fixed} routing distribution, whereas TACET's distribution
\emph{shifts downward over time} through online distillation. Our
experiments include a static cascade as a baseline precisely to isolate
this effect.

\paragraph{Cascades that learn over time.} Two recent lines share TACET's
goal of a cascade whose cost falls as the workload streams. Online cascade
learning~\citep{onlinecascade2024} imitates the LLM with a cheap neural
student trained on the stream; the distilled artifact is student
\emph{parameters}. Inter-Cascade~\citep{intercascade2025} extracts reusable
natural-language strategies from strong-model answers and retrieves them
into the weak model's context, cutting strong-model calls by up to 48\%;
the artifact is \emph{text}, applied in-context. TACET's artifact is a set
of executable, type-checked Datalog rules and facts: it answers without any
neural model in the loop, generalises to unseen entities through deductive
closure, and every Tier-1 answer carries a replayable proof tree.
LLM2LAS~\citep{llm2las2025} also turns LLM output into logic rules (via
ILASP) but offline, outside any cost-accounted cascade;
NeSyC~\citep{nesyc2025} continually refines LLM-hypothesised rules for
embodied agents. Compile-once neuro-symbolic offloading in the legal
domain~\citep{legaloffload2026} and reasoning-to-program
compilation~\citep{reacomp2026} share the amortisation telos but translate
top-down in a single pass, rather than mining incrementally from teacher
answers under an ontology gate. A recent survey~\citep{expcompression2026}
places declarative rules at the maximal-compression end of agent experience
and identifies precisely this design point as under-explored.

\paragraph{KGQA.} Embedding-based KGQA answers multi-hop questions in a
learned vector space~\citep{saxena2020embedkgqa}. A recent line uses an LLM
as an agent that walks the graph: Think-on-Graph~\citep{sun2024tog} and
Reasoning-on-Graphs~\citep{luo2024rog} interleave LLM reasoning with graph
retrieval for faithfulness. These systems make every query an LLM
interaction; they improve \emph{quality}, not \emph{cost}, and they do not
accumulate reusable knowledge. TACET is complementary: such an agent could
serve as our Tier-3 teacher. Closest in benchmark terms, recent work
combines LLM planning with embedding-guided search for efficient multi-hop
QA on MetaQA~\citep{effmultihop2025}, but the pipeline is static: nothing
learned while answering one query reduces the cost of the next.

\paragraph{KG embeddings and rule mining.} TransE~\citep{bordes2013transe},
ComplEx~\citep{trouillon2016complex}, RotatE~\citep{sun2019rotate} and
relational GNNs~\citep{schlichtkrull2018rgcn} predict missing edges from
graph structure; they are our Tier-2. AMIE~\citep{galarraga2015amie} mines
Horn rules from a knowledge base by support and confidence. Our rule miner
(\texttt{tacet.distill.distill.mine\_rules}) is a deliberately simple
length-$\le 2$ nested-loop miner that illustrates the distillation step; it
is \emph{not} a reimplementation of AMIE/AMIE+ --- it has no prefix-tree
pruning and no confidence bounds --- and we make no claim of superiority
over AMIE/AMIE+. Our
contribution is the \emph{setting}: mining rules from \emph{teacher answers}
as a workload streams, and feeding them to a downstream sound reasoner.
ChatRule~\citep{chatrule2023} prompts an LLM to propose logical rules for
KG completion offline and ranks them with AMIE-style support and
confidence; TACET instead mines rules online from teacher answers during
serving, and installs them only after they pass an ontology type-gate.

\paragraph{Temporal knowledge graphs and graph world models.} A mature line
forecasts future edges of a temporal knowledge graph from its history:
RE-NET~\citep{jin2020renet} and RE-GCN~\citep{li2021regcn} couple recurrent
encoders with relational GNNs, EvoNet~\citep{wu2020evonet} predicts the
next-step graph topology directly, and more recent methods reason over the
history with LLM chains~\citep{chainhistory2024} or learned rule
confidences~\citep{counttrucola2025}; ICEWS14 is the standard benchmark, on
which results are notoriously protocol-sensitive~\citep{gastinger2022evaluation}.
Recent surveys cast such models as \emph{graph world
models}~\citep{liu2025gfm,liu2026gwmtaxonomy}, distinguishing forward
dynamic simulation from logical/causal reasoning. We experimented with a
learned forward-dynamics head in this space (Section~\ref{sec:layer4}), but
it does not match purpose-built temporal models, and we report it as a
negative result (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) rather than a contribution.

\paragraph{Neuro-symbolic reasoning engines.}
DeepProbLog~\citep{manhaeve2018deepproblog} integrates logic with neural
perception, and a recent wave of systems couples LLMs with a symbolic
backbone for knowledge-graph reasoning: CLAUSE~\citep{clause2025} treats
neuro-symbolic KG reasoning as agentic context engineering, while
Delta1~\citep{delta1llm2026} integrates symbolic and neural components for
explainable inference. CLAUSE is the closest neighbour to this work --- it
is cost- and latency-budgeted neuro-symbolic KGQA evaluated on MetaQA, the
same benchmark family we use --- but it budgets evidence \emph{per query}
with a learned controller and does not persist learned structure across the
stream, whereas TACET's contribution is the opposite axis: \emph{cross-stream}
distillation that installs reusable rules so later queries skip the LLM
entirely. We position against it rather than re-implement its per-query
controller, and a head-to-head budgeted comparison is left to future work.
Systematic surveys confirm a crowded field~\citep{nsreview2025,nskgsurvey2024}. TACET is neuro-symbolic at the
\emph{systems} level: it does not differentiate through logic but composes a
symbolic engine, an embedding model and an LLM, and lets the neural tier
\emph{teach} the symbolic tier executable rules at run time. The
differentiator is the online distillation loop --- the neural tier installs
checkable rules into the sound symbolic tier as the workload streams ---
rather than a one-shot composition with a fixed routing distribution.

\paragraph{Auditable and proof-carrying reasoning.} A line of work asks
that a system's answer arrive with a verifiable trace.
Proof-carrying neuro-symbolic code attaches machine-checkable certificates to
neuro-symbolic programs~\citep{proofcarrying2025}, and auditable
neuro-symbolic pipelines have been proposed in high-stakes domains such as
pathology, where an explicit query is exposed as the trace of
evidence~\citep{auditpathology2026}. These works argue for auditability in
the abstract or for a specific application surface; TACET is a concrete
Datalog instantiation of the idea, producing a per-answer proof tree --- the
minimal set of ground rule firings that derives each committed answer ---
that can be replayed independently of the model that proposed it.

\paragraph{Neuro-symbolic compliance and privacy reasoning.} Regulatory
compliance is a natural home for proof-carrying neuro-symbolic reasoning, and a
recent line attacks it directly. PrivaCI-Bench~\citep{privacibench2025} frames
GDPR/HIPAA compliance as contextual-integrity reasoning and is the benchmark we
adopt for our second domain. GraphCompliance~\citep{graphcompliance2025} aligns
policy and context graphs for LLM-based compliance assessment, and
Cosmic~\citep{cosmicgdpr2026} performs automated reasoning over GDPR consent
violations; both translate a fixed policy top-down, whereas TACET \emph{mines}
the compliance rules online from a teacher's verdicts under an ontology gate.
ARIEL~\citep{arielprivacy2025} personalises agent privacy decisions by logical
entailment over prior user choices --- rule-based privacy reasoning, but not a
cost-amortising cascade. Closest in name, SELFCI~\citep{selfci2026} performs
\emph{self-distillation for contextual integrity}, but its student is the LLM's
own \emph{parameters}; TACET's student is a set of executable Datalog rules,
a cross-representation distillation whose artifact answers without any neural
model in the loop. Finally, a position paper argues that logical
\emph{soundness} alone is not a reliable correctness criterion for
neuro-symbolic fact-checking~\citep{soundnessnotreliable2026}; we agree, and it
is exactly why our soundness guarantee (Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}) is scoped
to type-consistency and provenance rather than world-correctness, and why we
report the article-F1 over-generalisation honestly (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Causal reasoning over knowledge graphs.} Causal knowledge graphs
encode interventional and counterfactual structure to explain
predictions~\citep{causalkg2022}, and recent work routes LLM causal
reasoning through a knowledge-graph representation~\citep{causalkgllm2024} or
checks the correctness of LLM-produced causal chains \emph{after the fact}
with symbolic verification~\citep{doverifier2026}. TACET ships standalone
Pearl-style utility functions (back-door / front-door / instrumental-variable
adjustment and counterfactual evaluation) as an extension module
(Appendix~\ref{sec:implementation}); they are not invoked by the cascade and we
make no claim of a causal contribution here.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation and world models.} Classic
distillation~\citep{hinton2015distillation} transfers a teacher network's
soft predictions into a smaller student of the \emph{same} kind. TACET
performs \emph{cross-representation} distillation: the student of an LLM is
a set of logical rules and graph facts. Concretely, it mines
teacher-contributed graph facts into Horn rules in the style of
AMIE~\citep{galarraga2015amie} and then Datalog-checks them, rather than
translating LLM text directly into rules. Surveys of symbolic knowledge
distillation flag correctness-verification of the distilled rules as an open
problem~\citep{symdistill2024}; TACET closes this in its setting by using
the proof tree as the rule's correctness certificate --- a distilled rule is
admitted only when its firings derive answers through sound, replayable
derivations. The framing of a knowledge graph as
an editable, reasoning ``world model'' connects to work on world
models~\citep{ha2018worldmodels,lecun2022jepa} and to the Graph World
Model~\citep{feng2025gwm}, which unifies tasks as operations on a graph;
relative to that line we contribute a concrete cost-reducing mechanism and
an empirical cost study. Graph foundation models~\citep{liu2025gfm,
battaglia2018relational} are a natural future replacement for our Tier-2.

\section{The TACET Cascade}
\label{sec:cascade}

\paragraph{Problem setting.} The world state is a typed labeled-property
graph $G=(V,E,\tau)$ with entities $V$, directed typed edges
$E\subseteq V\times R\times V$, and a type map $\tau:V\to T$. An
\emph{ontology} $\mathcal{O}$ assigns each relation $r\in R$ a domain and
range over types and optional structural axioms (symmetric, transitive,
functional, inverse). A \emph{query} is a pair $(h,q)$ asking for the tail
set $\{t : (h,q,t)\text{ holds}\}$; the system processes a stream
$Q=\langle (h_1,q_1),\dots,(h_n,q_n)\rangle$. Each tier $i$ has a unit cost
$c_i$ with $c_1\!\ll\!c_2\!\ll\!c_3$. The objective is to minimise the
blended cost $\sum_i c_{\mathrm{tier}(i)}$ subject to an accuracy target.

\paragraph{Tier 1: symbolic.} A rule is a function-free, range-restricted
Horn clause $b_1\wedge\dots\wedge b_k \Rightarrow h$. Given base facts $F$
and a rule set $\mathcal{R}$ (ontology axioms, engineer-written rules, and
rules synthesised at run time), forward chaining materialises the
\emph{deductive closure}
\[
  C(F,\mathcal{R}) \;=\; \mathrm{lfp}\;\big(X \mapsto F \cup
  \{\,\sigma(h)\;:\; (b\Rightarrow h)\in\mathcal{R},\;
  \sigma\models b \subseteq X\,\}\big).
\]
A query $(h,q)$ is answered by $\{t:(h,q,t)\in C\}$; if this is empty the
tier \emph{abstains}. Every derived triple stores the rule and premises
that produced it, so an answer unfolds into a full proof tree.

\paragraph{Tier 2: KGE.} ComplEx represents entities and relations as
complex vectors and scores a triple by the multilinear form
$\phi(h,r,t)=\mathrm{Re}\langle \mathbf{e}_h,\mathbf{w}_r,
\bar{\mathbf{e}}_t\rangle$. We train it with type-correct negative sampling
(corrupting a tail with another \emph{valid} tail of the same relation,
which is essential for the model to discriminate within a type) and a
binary cross-entropy loss. For a query, candidate tails are restricted to
the ontology range; the top candidate is returned with a confidence given
by a temperature-scaled softmax over candidate scores, the temperature
fitted on a held-out split~\citep{guo2017calibration}. A prediction is
accepted only if its confidence exceeds a threshold $\theta$ \emph{and} it
satisfies the ontology (domain, range, functionality); otherwise the tier
abstains.

\paragraph{Tier 3: LLM teacher.} The teacher answers any query. In a
deployment it is a frontier LLM, optionally a graph-walking agent
\citep{sun2024tog}; in our experiments it is an oracle over ground truth,
optionally noisy, so results are reproducible offline (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

\paragraph{Routing.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:route} routes a query to the first
tier that does not abstain. A Tier-2 prediction additionally passes the
Tier-1 ontology check before it is surfaced --- the neural tier proposes,
the symbolic tier vetoes.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{TACET query routing with distillation}
\label{alg:route}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{Ask}{$h,q$}
  \State $A \gets \textsc{SymbolicQuery}(h,q)$
  \If{$A \neq \varnothing$} \Return $(\text{tier }1,\,A)$ \EndIf
  \State $(t,\mathit{conf}) \gets \textsc{KgePredict}(h,q)$
  \If{$\mathit{conf}\ge\theta$ \textbf{and} $\textsc{OntologyAllows}(h,q,t)$}
     \State \Return $(\text{tier }2,\,\{t\})$
  \EndIf
  \State $A \gets \textsc{TeacherAnswer}(h,q)$
  \State \textsc{Distil}$(h,q,A)$ \Comment{Section~\ref{sec:distill}}
  \State \Return $(\text{tier }3,\,A)$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Online Distillation}
\label{sec:distill}

Each Tier-3 answer is compiled into reusable assets by three mechanisms.

\paragraph{(1) Fact write-back.} The triples $(h,q,t)$ are inserted as
graph edges and into the Tier-1 closure, so the \emph{identical} query is
thereafter answered by Tier~1 at cost $c_1$.

\paragraph{(2) Rule synthesis.} Write-back alone behaves like a cache --- it
helps only exact repeats. To generalise, TACET mines Horn rules. The
teacher returns the \emph{complete} answer set for each head it is asked, so
after a relation $q$ has been queried for a set $H_q$ of \emph{complete
heads}, we mine candidate rules $b\Rightarrow q(x,y)$ with bodies of length
one or two over the base relations (and $q$ itself, enabling recursion). A
candidate's confidence is the fraction of body groundings $(x,y)$ with
$x\in H_q$ that also satisfy the head; estimating confidence only over
complete heads avoids penalising a correct rule for as-yet-unobserved heads.
Rules with confidence $\ge\gamma$ and support $\ge\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$ are installed in
Tier~1. Each mined rule carries an \emph{inequality guard} $x\neq y$:
because confidence was measured on non-reflexive pairs, the guard forbids
the rule from deriving reflexive triples (e.g.\ $\mathit{ancestor}(x,x)$)
that the measurement never sanctioned --- without it, a compositional rule
combined with a symmetric premise produces spurious self-loops.

The miner is a deliberately simple nested enumeration
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:mine}), not an AMIE+ reimplementation: it has no
prefix-tree pruning and no confidence bounds, only a direct length-one and
length-two scan over the allowed relations, scored on the complete heads.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{AMIE-style rule miner (\texttt{tacet.distill.mine\_rules})}
\label{alg:mine}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{MineRules}{$G,\,F_{\mathrm{teacher}},\,q,\,H_q,\,B,\,\gamma,\,\sigma_{\mathrm{min}},\,m$}
  \State \textbf{Input:} graph $G$; teacher facts $F_{\mathrm{teacher}}$; target
  relation $q$; complete heads $H_q$; allowed body relations $B$; confidence
  $\gamma$, support $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$, cap $m$
  \State $F \gets \textsc{Triples}(G)\cup F_{\mathrm{teacher}}$;\quad
  index $I[r]\gets\{(x,y):(x,r,y)\in F\}$ for each relation $r$
  \State $T \gets I[q]$ \Comment{target pairs}
  \If{$|T| < \sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$} \Return $\varnothing$ \EndIf
  \State $\textsc{keep}(P)\gets\{(x,y)\in P : x\in H_q\}$
  \Comment{score only on complete heads}
  \State $\mathcal{S} \gets \mathrm{sort}(\,(B\cup\{q\})\cap \mathrm{dom}(I)\,)$;\quad
  $C \gets \varnothing$
  \For{$r_1\in\mathcal{S}$ with $r_1\neq q$, \textbf{each} $\mathit{inv}_1\in\{\bot,\top\}$}
    \Comment{length-1 body}
    \State $P \gets \textsc{keep}(\textsc{Dir}(I[r_1],\mathit{inv}_1))$
    \If{$|P|\ge\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$}
      \State $s\gets|P\cap T|$;\quad $c\gets s/|P|$
      \If{$c\ge\gamma$ \textbf{and} $s\ge\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$}
        $C\gets C\cup\{\langle r_1^{\mathit{inv}_1}\!\Rightarrow q,\,c,\,s\rangle\}$
      \EndIf
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \For{$r_1,\mathit{inv}_1$ \textbf{and} $r_2,\mathit{inv}_2$ over $\mathcal{S}\times\{\bot,\top\}$}
    \Comment{length-2 body}
    \State $A_1\gets\textsc{Adj}(\textsc{Dir}(I[r_1],\mathit{inv}_1))$;\quad
    $A_2\gets\textsc{Adj}(\textsc{Dir}(I[r_2],\mathit{inv}_2))$
    \State $P \gets \textsc{keep}(\{(x,y): \exists z,\ z\in A_1[x],\ y\in A_2[z],\ x\neq y\})$
    \If{$|P|\ge\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$}
      \State $s\gets|P\cap T|$;\quad $c\gets s/|P|$
      \If{$c\ge\gamma$ \textbf{and} $s\ge\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$}
        $C\gets C\cup\{\langle (r_1^{\mathit{inv}_1}\!\circ r_2^{\mathit{inv}_2})\!\Rightarrow q,\,c,\,s\rangle\}$
      \EndIf
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State sort $C$ by $(c,s)$ descending; drop duplicate rule names
  \State \Return first $m$ rules of $C$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{(3) KGE augmentation.} Teacher facts join the Tier-2 training
set; at the next \emph{consolidation} the embedding is warm-started on the
enriched graph, so it generalises from teacher answers to structurally
similar entities.

\paragraph{Consolidation.} Write-back and rule synthesis take effect
immediately; KGE augmentation is applied in periodic \emph{consolidation}
steps (a warm-started re-fit), modelling a cheap background batch job.

\paragraph{Soundness.} Let $\mathcal{R}$ be the active rule set and $F$ the
base facts.

\begin{proposition}[Tier-1 soundness]
\label{prop:sound}
Forward chaining over function-free, range-restricted Horn rules computes
the least fixpoint $C(F,\mathcal{R})$, which equals the set of triples
entailed by $F\cup\mathcal{R}$ under Datalog semantics. Hence every answer
TACET returns from Tier~1 is entailed by $F\cup\mathcal{R}$; Tier~1 never
returns an unentailed triple, and abstains otherwise.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:sound} is soundness \emph{relative to the rule set}.
It does \emph{not} claim a synthesised rule is correct about the world: a
mining error or a wrong teacher answer can install a flawed rule. The
confidence threshold $\gamma$, the support threshold $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$, the
complete-head estimator, the inequality guard, and the ontology type check
all reduce this risk; Section~\ref{sec:audit} (E6) measures the residual
effect of a noisy teacher empirically.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{A controlled benchmark.} We generate a synthetic
``organisation-and-society'' knowledge graph: people, companies, cities,
regions, departments, skills and languages, linked by base relations
(employment, residence, geography, management, friendship, family,
skills). Derived and held-out relations form a query workload in which
\emph{every query has a known answerability class}:
\begin{description}
  \item[\textsc{Stated}] a base fact present in the graph --- Tier~1
  answers it directly.
  \item[\textsc{Ded-Given}] entailed by a rule shipped with the system
  (transitive-closure rules for \texttt{ancestor\_of}) --- Tier~1.
  \item[\textsc{Ded-Discover}] entailed by a rule the teacher must
  \emph{synthesise} (\texttt{superior\_of}, the transitive closure of
  management; \texttt{colleague\_of}, a relational composition) --- Tier~3
  until the rule is mined, Tier~1 thereafter.
  \item[\textsc{Inductive}] {\sloppy a withheld edge with no entailing rule but
  strong structural regularity (\texttt{primary\_language}, which clusters
  by employer with noise) --- Tier-2 territory.\par}
  \item[\textsc{Novel}] an idiosyncratic attribute with no graph signal ---
  Tier~3 only; exact repeats become Tier~1 via write-back.
\end{description}
The workload mixes the five classes and interleaves repeats at a
controllable rate. This design lets us attribute every routing decision and
every cost saving to a known cause.

\paragraph{Why synthetic, why an oracle teacher.} A synthetic generator
gives exact control over the variables the cost dynamics depend on ---
class mix, repeat rate, graph size, teacher error --- and exact ground
truth for every answerability class, which a natural-language benchmark
cannot. Modelling the teacher as an oracle removes LLM answer noise as a
confound; experiment E6 then \emph{re-introduces} it as a controlled
variable. The price is external validity, which we address in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{Baselines.} \textbf{LLM-only} sends every query to the teacher
(the cost ceiling). \textbf{Symbolic-only} uses just the shipped rules and
abstains otherwise (the accuracy floor; measures rule coverage).
\textbf{Cache cascade} is Tier~1 $+$ an exact-match answer cache $+$ the
teacher --- the honest ``just memoise'' baseline. \textbf{Static cascade}
is the full three-tier cascade with distillation switched off. \textbf{TACET}
is the full system.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Each experiment runs \seeds{} independent benchmark
instances (distinct seeds); we report the mean and a 95\% confidence
interval. The default workload is 300 queries with a 30\% repeat rate;
TACET consolidates every 100 queries. Tier costs are
$c_1{=}10^{-4},c_2{=}10^{-3},c_3{=}5{\times}10^{-2}$ USD; the qualitative
results depend only on $c_3\!\gg\!c_2\!>\!c_1$. ComplEx uses $64$
dimensions; mining uses $\gamma{=}0.95,\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}{=}3$ and triggers after $10$
complete heads. Full hyper-parameters and the KGE link-prediction quality
are in the appendix.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Deductive Reasoning with Proofs (ProofWriter)}

Each ProofWriter \emph{theory} is a handful of facts and IF-THEN rules together
with boolean questions, every question labelled with a gold answer \emph{and} a
proof chain of known depth (\texttt{QDep}). We map each fact
\texttt{(arg0, verb, arg1, +)} to an edge \texttt{arg0}\,--\texttt{verb}-->\texttt{arg1},

\paragraph{Expressivity boundary (stated honestly).} TACET's symbolic tier is
\emph{positive} Datalog. ProofWriter-CWA expresses the queried answer's polarity
on the question (handled by negation-as-failure at query time), but a subset of
theories also use negation-as-failure \emph{inside a rule body} (e.g.\ ``if
someone is white and not blue then they are young''). Such rules are outside
positive Datalog, so we \emph{exclude} those theories and \emph{report} the
excluded fraction rather than silently dropping or mishandling them: across the
five depths this is \pwExclPct{}\% of theories ($\pwExclFrac{}$ of the sampled
$1{,}500$). The remaining, expressible theories are evaluated in full.

\paragraph{Reading the result.} On the expressible subset the engine attains
$\pwAccOverall{}$ accuracy and $\pwProofVal{}$ proof-validity at every depth,
against a majority-class baseline of $0.500$. \textbf{We emphasise that this
$1.000$ is an \emph{expected sound-and-complete ceiling}, not a competitive
accuracy claim}: ProofWriter-CWA
gold labels are themselves the forward-chaining closure of facts under rules, so
a \emph{sound and complete} positive-Datalog engine answers every expressible
question correctly --- depth $0$ through $5$ alike, since forward chaining
reaches the least fixpoint regardless of proof length. The point of the
experiment is therefore not a novel accuracy number. It is the
\pwProofCov{}-coverage, \pwProofVal{}-validity column: \emph{every} deductive
answer ships a proof tree that grounds, step by step, in the theory's base facts
and named rules (Section~\ref{sec:audit}). The contribution TACET makes on this
task is the \emph{checkable proof} accompanying each answer --- the property a
regulated or auditable deployment needs --- not leaderboard accuracy on a suite
where the gold standard is, by construction, a closure the engine computes
exactly.

\section{Auditable Reasoning: Proof Trees and Their Evaluation}
\label{sec:audit}

Every answer the symbolic tier returns carries its \emph{proof tree}: the rule
applied and the (recursively annotated) base or earlier-derived facts that
matched its body. Expanding any tree to its leaves terminates in base facts of
the world graph --- a corollary of the soundness of forward chaining
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sound}) --- so a downstream consumer can ask TACET for
the provenance of any Tier-1 answer and receive an explanation in terms of
facts a human authored or admitted. Nothing is invented.

We quantify this with two metrics over the symbolic results
(\texttt{tacet.eval.eval\_audit}). \emph{Proof coverage} is the share of workload
queries that are answered \emph{and} ship a non-empty proof. \emph{Proof
validity} is, for an answered query, the fraction of its proof that is
\emph{grounded}: every derivation step reduces, through the proof tree, to a
base fact or a rule registered in the engine (an ontology axiom or a
synthesised/given rule), with no dangling or unknown-rule step. We stress that
proof validity audits the \emph{provenance integrity} of the proof the engine
produced --- that nothing unsupported entered the derivation --- not the
ground-truth correctness of the answer; a fully grounded proof of a wrong fact
still scores $1.0$. Correctness against the oracle is reported separately by the
accuracy column of Table~\ref{tab:main}.

On the synthetic-org benchmark (seed $0$, a $300$-query workload), the symbolic
tier answers the rule-covered fraction and abstains on the rest, giving a proof
coverage of \proofCoverage{} (Table~\ref{tab:audit}); every proof it produces is

intended one --- the engine only ever cites rules it holds and facts it was
given --- and the metric is the executable check that this invariant holds end
to end, the property an auditor or regulated deployment needs. A value below
$1.0$ would flag a proof that cites a retired or unregistered rule, or a
dangling derivation step; reporting it makes the soundness invariant an
executable, regression-checkable property rather than a one-off empirical
finding.

\subsection{Rule-mining precision: proposed vs.\ installed vs.\ world-correct}
\label{sec:rule_precision}

Proof validity audits provenance, not world-correctness: a soundly-applied but
\emph{wrong} synthesised rule still scores $1.0$
(Section~\ref{sec:distill}). A reviewer rightly asked us to quantify the gap. The
AMIE-style miner \emph{over-produces}: it forms a candidate body for every
length-one and length-two relation path, scores each on the teacher-answered
(``complete'') heads, and installs those clearing the confidence threshold
$\gamma$ and support $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$. Two questions follow. How aggressive is the
filter --- how many of the proposed candidates does it keep? And is the
\emph{confidence} estimate (a partial-data statistic over the answered heads) a
reliable proxy for being \emph{true in the world}? We answer both with three
counts (\texttt{tacet.eval.rule\_precision}):
\textbf{proposed}, the number of candidate bodies the miner forms before the
cut; \textbf{installed}, the rules that pass $\gamma,\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$, dedup and the cap
(what the cascade adds to Tier~1); and \textbf{world-correct}, the installed
rules whose precision on the \emph{full} ground-truth graph is $\ge 0.9$. World
precision of $b\Rightarrow q(x,y)$ is the fraction of $(x,y)$ pairs satisfying
$b$ that also satisfy $q$, measured over \emph{every} entity in the ground-truth
graph --- not only the answered heads the confidence filter saw --- so it
exposes a rule that fits the observed heads yet over-fires elsewhere.

On the synthetic benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:rule_precision}), aggregated over
\ruleSeeds{} seeds ($0$--$4$), the miner proposes \ruleProposed{} candidate
bodies across the discoverable relations; the confidence threshold installs
\ruleInstalled{} of them --- a ${\sim}94\%$ rejection rate, confirming the
over-production the reviewer flagged. Of the installed rules, \ruleWorldCorrect{}
are world-correct ($\ge 0.9$ precision on the full graph) at a mean installed
precision of \ruleMeanPrec{}. Crucially, the filter is \emph{not} watertight, and
the way it leaks is seed-dependent: across the five seeds, \emph{every}
non-world-correct installed rule is a \texttt{primary\_language} rule, the
\textsc{Inductive} relation that clusters by employer \emph{with noise}. There,
``shares a manager $\Rightarrow$ shares a language'' fits the answered heads
well enough to clear $\gamma{=}0.95$ but holds for only ${\sim}0.75$--$0.83$ of
all pairs, because the homophily is statistical, not logical. The leak is
intermittent: in $2$ of $5$ seeds the cascade accumulates enough complete heads
for \texttt{primary\_language} to synthesise a rule at all, and only those seeds
incur a sub-$0.9$ rule (driving mean precision to $0.973$ and $0.988$); in the
other $3$ seeds no \texttt{primary\_language} rule clears the filter and mean
precision is a clean $1.000$. The non-zero std ($\pm 0.010$ on precision,
$\pm 0.5$ on world-correct count) is therefore exactly this near-functional
relation flickering in and out of the installed set, not noise in the deductive
relations, which are world-correct on every seed. This is exactly the failure
mode the soundness caveat in Section~\ref{sec:distill} anticipates: Tier~1
applies these rules soundly and they are still wrong about the world. We report
it plainly rather than hide it --- the confidence filter is a strong but leaky
guard, and a noisy, near-functional relation is where it leaks. On the
committed MetaQA-2hop \emph{oracle} run, by contrast, the single installed
composition rule (\texttt{\char`\~starred\_actors\,.\,directed\_by}) is exactly
the target 2-hop relation, so its world precision is
\oracleRuleMeanPrec{} (\oracleRuleWorldCorrect{}/\oracleRuleInstalled{}
world-correct): when the latent rule is genuinely deductive, the miner installs
it cleanly. The takeaway is the honest one --- the filter removes the vast
majority of spurious candidates and recovers genuine deductive compositions
perfectly, but it is a statistical confidence test, so on relations with
near-functional noise a minority of installed rules generalise imperfectly;
correctness-verifying installed rules against held-out data (rather than only
the answered heads) is the natural next guard.

\paragraph{$\gamma$-sensitivity: the precision/recall knob.}
Figure~\ref{fig:gamma} sweeps the miner's confidence threshold $\gamma$ over
$\{0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99\}$ (five seeds each, oracle teacher) and plots,
at each $\gamma$, the number of rules installed into Tier~1 and their mean
world-precision. As $\gamma$ rises the filter installs fewer rules but their mean
world-precision climbs toward $1.0$: the rules eliminated last, as the bar
tightens, are exactly the noisy \texttt{primary\_language} homophily rules whose
answered-head confidence sits just below the cut, so removing them is what lifts
world-precision. This is the precision/recall tradeoff of the confidence filter
made explicit --- the operating point $\gamma{=}0.95$ used throughout the paper
sits near the knee, keeping the deductive compositions while admitting only the
occasional near-functional rule.

\paragraph{The leaky rule is installed into Tier~1 (cost--accuracy caveat).}
The two $\sim\!0.75$-world-precision \texttt{primary\_language} rules of seed~$0$
are not discarded: they are installed into \emph{Tier~1}, the tier the paper
markets as sound, abstaining rather than guessing. So for that relation Tier~1
now answers ${\sim}25\%$ \emph{wrong} while \emph{simultaneously} carrying
proof-validity $1.0$ --- the answers are soundly derived from the installed rule
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sound}) and yet wrong about the world. The danger is that
this gap is invisible at runtime: the miner's confidence estimate for these rules
exceeds even $\gamma{=}0.99$ (measured on the answered heads it saw --- the
sweep installs them unchanged at every threshold up to $0.99$), but their
world-precision is ${\sim}0.75$, and that ${\sim}18$--$24$-point gap cannot be detected online because
world-precision requires ground truth the system does not have. The cascade does
ship an instrument that could retire such rules ---
\texttt{tacet.experimental.episodic.FeedbackCurator} demotes a rule's trust on
negative user feedback and retires it below a threshold --- but it is
\emph{experimental and not wired into the evaluated cascade}, so the reported runs
carry the leaky rules to the end. The natural guard, and concrete future work, is
to gate rule \emph{installation} on a held-out precision estimate (a fresh slice
of heads the confidence statistic never saw) rather than on answered-head
confidence alone, turning the offline world-precision check of this section into
an online admission test. This guard is now realized and measured:
Section~\ref{sec:admission} gates held-out candidates at admission --- every
leaky candidate is rejected in all cells with a populated leakage class, at
the price of teacher- and seed-dependent composition keep-rates. This is the operational face of the soundness caveat in
Section~\ref{sec:distill}: Proposition~\ref{prop:sound} guarantees soundness
\emph{relative to the rule set}, not that an installed rule is correct about the
world, and a near-functional relation is precisely where that distinction bites.

\subsection{Efficiency as a Side Effect of Distillation}
\label{sec:efficiency}

The same distillation loop that turns teacher answers into checkable Horn
rules also migrates the regular majority of a workload off the teacher and
onto the cheap symbolic and embedding tiers. The cost reduction that follows
is therefore not a separately engineered optimisation but a direct
consequence of the auditable design: every query that clears at Tier~1/2
does so because a \emph{grounded, validity-checked} rule or embedding already
covers it. We report the efficiency numbers here, after the reasoning and
auditability results, to keep them in their proper place --- the
${\sim}\costFactor\times$ blended-cost reduction is what auditability buys,
not what the system is for.

\paragraph{E1: main comparison.} Table~\ref{tab:main} compares all five
systems. \textbf{The cost figures in this grid come from a \emph{simulated}
constant per-tier cost ($c_1{=}10^{-4}$, $c_2{=}10^{-3}$,
$c_3{=}5{\times}10^{-2}$ USD) applied to a synthetic workload with an
author-specified class mix; they are not measured against a real LLM bill.}
Under that cost model TACET attains a blended cost of \$\akCost{} against
\$\llmCost{} for LLM-only --- a ${\sim}\costFactor\times$ reduction --- at
\akAcc{} accuracy. It is also cheaper than the cache cascade (\$\cacheCost{}):
caching only amortises exact repeats, whereas TACET's synthesised rules
and trained embedding answer \emph{unseen} entities, so a far larger share
of the workload clears at Tier~1/2. Symbolic-only is nearly free but
answers only the rule-covered fraction; the static cascade pays the teacher
repeatedly for every distinct hard query. The real-data corroboration of this
trajectory is Section~\ref{sec:realcost}.

only the long tail (${\sim}24\%$ at $\varepsilon=0$), and the symbolic
and embedding tiers --- whose answers are not produced by the teacher ---
are insensitive to teacher noise on the majority of queries. Distillation
can still propagate teacher errors (a wrong fact written back, or a rule
mined from inconsistent answers), but the confidence/support thresholds
reject most spurious rules, and write-back types each new entity from the
relation's declared schema so a synthesised rule cannot silently derive an
untyped fact (a type-inconsistent fact between existing entities is still
possible and is surfaced by the validator, not silently admitted). Cost rises slightly with $\varepsilon$ (\$\(3.8\!\to\!4.6\))
because inconsistent teacher answers depress mined-rule confidence below
threshold, so fewer queries migrate down. In a deployment with a
genuinely unreliable teacher, gating write-back and synthesis on a
voting/agreement check is the natural mitigation.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\noindent\emph{Reading guide:} the failure-mode inventory
(Appendix~\ref{sec:failure_modes}) and the extension-boundary appendix
(\ref{sec:boundary}) complement this discussion; together the three sections
delimit when the cascade does and does not pay off.

\paragraph{External validity.} The main grid is synthetic with an oracle
teacher --- a deliberate choice that buys exact ground truth and control over
the causal variables, the appropriate first study for a routing/distillation
mechanism. Section~\ref{sec:realcost} complements it with a real Grok~4.3
teacher on the real-entity MetaQA KG, confirming the measured cost
amortisation ($2.8\times$/$5.1\times$ pooled over 3 seeds for TACET). That real-LLM study also surfaces the main
external-validity caveat, which we state precisely: the cost amortisation we
claim is delivered by the caching tier and survives intact under the real
teacher ($2.8\times$/$5.1\times$); what is gated on teacher quality is only the
\emph{additional} rule-generalisation advantage \emph{over} caching, which on
noisy real-LLM compositional workloads collapses so that the cascade reduces
to a cache. Broader natural-language KGQA benchmarks (WebQSP, CWQ)
with diverse real teachers remain valuable future work, and the released code
exposes a single-function teacher interface for them.

\paragraph{Ontology preservation under write-back.} Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}
guarantees that the symbolic closure is ontology-consistent \emph{given} that
the graph and the installed rules are. The engine maintains the rule premise
by a type-consistency check at \texttt{add\_rule}, and the write-back path
types each new entity with a type the relation's declared domain/range admits.
Under the default \texttt{induce} schema this domain/range is the \emph{set} of
observed endpoint types, so a multi-type relation contributes a representative
member of that set and a self-loop is typed from the domain--range intersection;
either way a teacher fact about a fresh entity keeps the graph well-typed. The
residual gap is a teacher
that asserts a type-inconsistent fact between \emph{existing} entities (a
mistyped answer in the noisy regime): write-back does not relabel an existing
node, so such a fact can enter the closure. We treat this as a teacher-quality
concern rather than a structural one --- it is detectable by the same
\texttt{ontology.validate} pass used in our audits --- and we report it
explicitly rather than claim the closure is type-safe unconditionally.

\paragraph{Forward graph dynamics: an honest negative result.}
\label{sec:layer4}
Forecasting the next graph state is outside the cascade's envelope --- its
value is sound deductive reasoning and cost amortisation, not entity-level
prediction --- and we establish that edge with a measurement rather than
assert it. We extended the engine with a learned forward-dynamics head ---
a Dreamer-V3-style recurrent latent mounted on the frozen Tier-2 ComplEx
embeddings --- to forecast the next graph state ($G_{t+1}$) on ICEWS14, the
standard temporal knowledge-graph benchmark. It is not competitive. The
released \texttt{layer4\_eval.json} reports three protocols on this head: a
sampled-candidate filtered MRR of $0.451$, a no-latent ablation of $0.395$,
and --- the honest number for comparison against the literature --- the
standard all-entity, time-aware full-ranking MRR of $0.0925$ (Hits@10
$0.185$). We report the full-ranking figure: $0.0925$, well below strong
temporal-KG forecasters such as RE-GCN ($\sim$$0.42$) and TiRGN with
Chain-of-History reasoning ($\sim$$0.44$)~\citep{chainhistory2024}. We tried
to close the gap and
report the result plainly: DreamerV3-style latent free-bits, backpropagation
through time, and action-as-node event encoding all failed to move the
comparable metric over the simplest configuration, across multiple Modal
training runs. We read this as a structural boundary rather than a tuning
failure: a sound positive-symbolic substrate is ill-suited to entity-level
temporal forecasting, where purpose-built temporal GNNs win by design. We
therefore report the forecaster as a \emph{limit} of the approach, not a
contribution --- the cascade's value is auditable deductive \emph{reasoning}
(sound, replayable proofs) and cost amortisation through distillation, not
forecasting accuracy. The forecaster code remains in the released
implementation for reproducibility and provenance.

\paragraph{Tier-2 model.} We use a CPU NumPy ComplEx. A production system
would substitute a GPU relational GNN (R-GCN, RotatE) behind the same
interface; this would raise Tier-2 accuracy and shift the E3 frontier
favourably but does not change the mechanism.

\paragraph{Rule language.} The miner searches Horn rules of body length up
to two over base relations. Deeper or existential rules, numerical
comparisons, and temporal rules are out of scope; richer inductive logic
programming is compatible with the same distillation loop.

\paragraph{Soundness is relative.} Proposition~\ref{prop:sound} guarantees
Tier-1 answers are entailed by the current rule set, not that the rule set
is correct. A wrong synthesised rule is ``soundly'' applied yet wrong about
the world. Mitigations are empirical (E6); formal verification of
synthesised rules against held-out data is promising future work.

\paragraph{Bias propagation through distillation.} The distillation loop
mines rules from the teacher's answers, so a \emph{biased} teacher can
propagate spurious or socially-biased correlations directly into the
installed Horn rules. This failure is more insidious than a noisy-teacher
error, because a biased rule then answers at Tier~1 with a clean, fully
grounded proof tree (Section~\ref{sec:audit}), so that a biased inference can inherit the surface authority of a sound
derivation --- a risk we flag, and return to with concrete mitigations below. Our
existing guards are only partial here: the ontology check rejects
type-inconsistent rules, and the world-precision evaluation
(Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision}) catches rules that are wrong about the
world on the \emph{whole} graph --- but neither targets \emph{fairness}
specifically. A rule can be world-correct on the training graph yet socially
harmful: a demographic proxy that holds statistically across the observed
entities (much as the \texttt{primary\_language} homophily rule of
Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision} holds for ${\sim}0.8$ of pairs) would clear
both guards while encoding a protected-attribute correlation no auditor would
sanction. Concretely, three mitigations are future work: bias-aware rule
vetting that audits each installed rule against known protected-attribute
proxies; teacher debiasing or answer verification before write-back, so a
biased answer never enters the candidate pool; and human-in-the-loop rule
review for high-stakes deployments. These extend the same held-out admission
test proposed in Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision} --- gating rule
\emph{installation} on a fresh evaluation slice --- to cover fairness, not
only world-precision.

\paragraph{Rule-induced article over-generalisation (compliance).} On the
compliance domain (Section~\ref{sec:compliance}) the same over-generalisation
that the synthetic \texttt{primary\_language} rule exhibits
(Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision}) appears at the level of \emph{cited
articles}: a synthesised pattern$\to$article rule fires on cases where the typed
contextual-integrity pattern usually --- but not always --- implies the article,
so the full arm's article-level micro-F1 falls
(\privArtFOneFullGemini--\privArtFOneFullGrok{} vs
${\sim}\privArtFOneLlmGemini{}$ for LLM-only) even though \emph{verdict} accuracy
is preserved at the iso-accuracy control. The mitigation is the one already
proposed for rule precision (Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision}): gate rule
\emph{installation} on a held-out article-precision estimate rather than
answered-case confidence alone. We report the trade-off plainly: the cost
amortisation is real, and it is paid for in article-attribution recall, not in
verdict correctness.

\paragraph{Compliance inputs are annotated, not extracted (compliance).} The
compliance experiment (Section~\ref{sec:compliance}) takes each case's
contextual-integrity slots from the benchmark's annotations, normalised by a
fixed deterministic vocabulary; it does \emph{not} include a learned extractor
that recovers those slots from raw case text. The reported amortisation is
therefore the saving on the verdict-reasoning cost, holding contextual-integrity
extraction fixed and free. A deployed pipeline would add an extraction stage
whose dollar cost and error both enter the picture --- a noisy extractor could
inject wrong atoms into the miner --- so extending the cost accounting and the
noise analysis to a real extractor is future work, the compliance-domain
analogue of the noise-robust-mining direction below.

\paragraph{Noise-robust rule mining.} The key next step exposed by the
real-LLM evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:realcost}) is a rule miner that
recovers generalising composition rules from a \emph{noisy, incomplete}
teacher --- so that distillation's beyond-cache advantage survives an
imperfect LLM teacher rather than collapsing to cache-equivalent behaviour.

\paragraph{Engineering.} The graph store is in-memory; the symbolic and
graph operations map directly onto a production graph database, and the
consolidation step onto a scheduled batch job.

\subsection{Is TACET ``just a semantic cache''?}
\label{sec:semcache}

A semantic cache memoises past (query, answer) pairs and additionally
serves near-duplicate queries by embedding similarity. It is the natural
sceptical reading of any system that sits in front of an LLM and makes
repeated questions cheap. The reading deserves to be taken seriously ---
and rejected precisely.

Our \textbf{cache cascade} baseline is an \emph{exact-match} cache (rules
$+$ an exact-match answer store $+$ the LLM) --- the honest ``just
memoise'' baseline. We do not implement the stronger embedding-similarity
(semantic) cache; below we mark exactly where one would and would not
close the gap. Every cost result separates TACET
from the exact-match cache. The decisive evidence is E5 (Figure~\ref{fig:sens}): as the
workload repeat rate falls to zero --- every query distinct --- the
cache's cost climbs steeply toward the LLM-only regime (rising
${\sim}2.5\times$ across the sweep) because an exact-match cache can only
return an answer it has \emph{literally seen}. A semantic cache would blunt
this curve only insofar as the distinct queries are near-duplicates of
prior ones; against \emph{structurally novel} heads (the regime E5 sweeps
toward) it degrades just as the exact-match cache does. TACET's cost at
zero repetition is $58\%$ of the cache's ($1.7\times$ lower). A cache's value is
repetition; TACET's value survives its absence.

The gap is produced by what a cache structurally cannot do:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Compositional generalisation.} When the teacher answers
\texttt{superior\_of} for ten employees, the miner induces the \emph{rule}
$\mathit{manages}\circ\mathit{manages}\Rightarrow\mathit{superior\_of}$.
That rule answers \texttt{superior\_of} for \emph{every} employee in the
graph, including those never queried and never seen by the teacher. Neither
an exact-match nor an embedding-similarity (semantic) cache has any entry
for a structurally unseen head, so this gap is robust to upgrading the
cache from exact-match to semantic.
\item \textbf{Deductive inference.} Tier 1 returns the deductive
\emph{closure} --- facts that were never stored and never asked
(transitive ancestors, alliance-derived relations). A cache stores facts;
it does not derive them.
\item \textbf{Structural prediction.} Tier 2 predicts genuinely unseen
edges from graph structure; a cache recalls but cannot predict.
\item \textbf{Soundness and verification.} Tier-1 answers are provably
entailed and Tier-2 answers ontology-checked; a cache returns whatever it
stored, stale or wrong, unchecked.
\item \textbf{Editability.} Change one fact and the closure updates
consistently; a cache silently keeps every entry that depended on the old
value.
\end{itemize}

A semantic cache is, precisely, the \emph{degenerate special case} of
TACET with rule synthesis and Tier 2 disabled and only fact write-back
retained. The closest ablation row (the \texttt{no\_rule\_synth}
configuration of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) disables synthesis but
\emph{retains} Tier~2, so the gap it measures against full TACET isolates
rule synthesis \emph{given} Tier~2 --- a \emph{lower} bound on the value
beyond caching: the true degenerate cache also loses Tier~2. We concede the converse honestly: for the \textsc{Novel} class ---
idiosyncratic facts with no structure --- TACET's write-back genuinely
\emph{is} a cache and nothing more. The claim is not that TACET is never
a cache; it is that TACET is a cache \emph{plus} a deductive reasoner
\emph{plus} a structural learner, and the cache is the least interesting
part. Caching amortises \emph{computation} --- the same question, cheaper
next time. Distillation amortises \emph{knowledge} --- an entire class of
questions, cheap thereafter, because the generative rule behind them has
been recovered. It is the difference between memorising answers and
learning the law that produces them.

\section{Conclusion}

TACET shows that a neuro-symbolic KGQA cascade can \emph{self-distil}: an
online loop turns each LLM teacher answer into checkable Horn rules and graph
facts, so the routing distribution drifts toward the cheap tiers as a workload
streams, and every Tier-1 answer carries a Datalog proof tree that reduces to
base facts and named rules under an ontology-preservation guarantee
(Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}) that holds even for the online-synthesised rules.
But the headline contribution of this paper is not that the cascade is
\emph{cheaper} --- it is the \emph{boundary condition} that says \emph{when} its
distinctive mechanism pays off. We state that boundary plainly. The measured
cost-amortisation we report ($2.8\times$/$5.1\times$ pooled over 3 seeds versus LLM-only,
Section~\ref{sec:realcost}) is delivered by the \emph{cache tier alone}:
write-back amortises exact repeats, and that is enough to produce the dollar
savings. The paper's distinctive claim --- that \emph{rule distillation}
generalises to unseen heads and therefore exceeds a cache --- does \emph{not}
show a measurable advantage under a real, noisy LLM teacher on this benchmark.
What we contribute instead is the \emph{quantified teacher-quality boundary} that
separates the two regimes, together with the instruments needed to locate it. The
boundary is sharp: with an oracle teacher rule distillation is positive ($87\%$
fewer teacher calls than caching on unseen heads), whereas with a real Grok~4.3
teacher at ${\sim}22$--$54\%$ accuracy on $2$-hop compositions the rule miner degrades
and the distillation-beyond-caching effect is \emph{neutral}.
On a second domain the boundary is \emph{crossed by a real teacher}: streaming
GDPR compliance cases (PrivaCI-Bench) past three frontier teachers, online rule
distillation beats an exact-match cache by $1.5$--$2.1\times$ at matched verdict
accuracy with a ${\sim}10\%$ repeat ceiling (Section~\ref{sec:compliance}), so the
distillation-over-caching premium is not an oracle artefact --- it materialises
whenever a real teacher is accurate enough for the miner to recover generalising
rules, and an accurate compliance teacher is.
The instruments
that make this measurable rather than anecdotal are proof-validity (does Tier~1
answer only through sound, replayable derivations?), rule-precision (do installed
rules hold on the \emph{whole} world, not just the answered heads?), and
real-cost metering (every teacher token billed at the provider's published
price). A precise, negative-leaning boundary is itself a result: it tells a
practitioner that this architecture buys auditable cost-amortisation through
caching today, and that its rule-distillation premium is contingent on teacher
quality crossing a threshold these instruments can now measure. We also report,
candidly, a second non-win: a learned graph-forecasting extension does not beat
purpose-built temporal models, and we present it as a negative result rather than
a contribution. We hope the released benchmark, instruments, and implementation
make self-distilling, proof-carrying cascades --- and the teacher-quality
boundary that governs them --- a reproducible baseline for knowledge-graph
question answering.

\section{Reproducibility and Hyper-parameters}

The implementation is dependency-light (NumPy only for the model) and runs
on a CPU without API keys. The full grid --- experiments E1--E6, \seeds{}
seeds --- is reproduced by
\texttt{python -m tacet.eval.experiment} followed by
\texttt{python experiments/analyze.py}, which regenerates every figure and
table in this paper, including the result macros.

\paragraph{Benchmark.} 120 people, 8 companies, 6 departments, 12 skills,
4 languages, 6 regions; workload 300 queries; repeat rate 0.30; class mix
$(\textsc{Stated},\textsc{Given},\textsc{Discover},\textsc{Inductive},
\textsc{Novel})=(0.22,0.20,0.20,0.23,0.15)$; language noise 0.15.

\paragraph{ComplEx.} dimension 64, 70 training epochs at warm-up, 25 epochs
per warm-started consolidation, Adam (lr $0.05$), 6 type-correct negatives
plus 4 uniform negatives per positive (the type-correct pool teaches
within-type discrimination for the cascade; the uniform pool is needed for
the standard open-world link-prediction protocol), $L_2$ regularisation
$10^{-6}$. Confidence temperature fitted on a held-out language split.

\paragraph{Mining.} body length $\le 2$ over base relations; confidence
threshold $\gamma=0.95$; support threshold $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}=3$; synthesis triggers
after 10 complete heads per relation; every mined rule carries the
$x\neq y$ guard.

\paragraph{Tier-2 link-prediction quality.} The standard filtered KGC
protocol on the synthetic benchmark (80/10/10 random split, 1{,}162 train /
145 valid / 146 test triples) at the hyper-parameters above, with the
standalone script's default of $8$ relation-pool negatives per positive as
recorded in the run config, attains
\textbf{MRR\,$\approx 0.34$, Hits@1\,$\approx 0.23$, Hits@3\,$\approx 0.38$,
Hits@10\,$\approx 0.60$} with the NumPy backend
(\texttt{results/kge\_synthetic\_numpy.json}, regenerated by
\texttt{python experiments/kge\_real\_eval.py --dataset synthetic --epochs 70}),
and \textbf{MRR\,$\approx 0.43$, Hits@10\,$\approx 0.69$} at $\mathrm{dim}=128$
with the PyTorch backend (\texttt{results/kge\_synthetic\_torch.json}).
Ranking peaks near MRR\,$0.34$ at the 70-epoch warm-up used by the cascade; we
report this warm-up operating point that the cascade actually consolidates from
rather than a longer standalone training run.
The cascade only \emph{accepts} a Tier-2 prediction above its calibrated
confidence threshold, so its operating accuracy on the cascade workload is
substantially higher than the raw top-1 ranking number.

benchmark.

\section{Implementation and productionization}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Substrate Extensions: Ingestion and Dataset Loaders}
\label{sec:tier_a}

The evaluation harness ships with two substrate extensions. None changes the cascade's results but each broadens the data the
cascade can operate on. (Standalone modules that are \emph{not} invoked by the
cascade --- temporal, causal, episodic, planner, federation, world-model ---
are described in the next subsections.)

\paragraph{Auto-ingestion from text.}
\texttt{tacet.core.ingest} provides an \texttt{Extractor} protocol with a
deterministic regex/\texttt{Pattern} implementation for tests and demos
and a \texttt{CallableExtractor} that wraps any
\texttt{(text) $\to$ list[triple]} function --- the single integration
point for a real LLM-based extractor (Gemini / Grok / GPT, or a
GraphRAG-style pipeline~\citep{edge2024graphrag}). \texttt{KGBuilder}
orchestrates extraction across a corpus and (when an ontology is given)
rejects triples whose relation is undeclared or whose endpoints violate
the type / functional constraints, with a per-corpus
\texttt{IngestionReport}. This closes the gap between ``hand-curated KG''
and ``any document set'' --- the cascade itself is unchanged.

\paragraph{Standard KGC dataset loaders.}
\texttt{tacet.data} adds a \texttt{KGDataset} dataclass and loaders
for the standard FB15k-237 / WN18RR / YAGO3-10 / NELL-995 layout
(\texttt{train.txt} / \texttt{valid.txt} / \texttt{test.txt}), a
\texttt{split\_triples} helper, the curated 15-country world-geography
graph used by the demo, and a \texttt{synthetic\_kg\_dataset} that exposes
the benchmark generator's graph as a KGC split. The
\texttt{kge\_real\_eval} experiment script reports standard filtered
MRR / Hits@$k$ on any of these. Plugging in FB15k-237 etc.\ is a single
flag (\texttt{--root path/to/FB15k-237}).
Both external corpora are fetched by checked-in scripts: the FB15k-237 /
WN18RR / YAGO3-10 splits via \texttt{scripts/fetch\_kgc\_data.sh}, and the
official ProofWriter V2020.12.3 release via
\texttt{scripts/fetch\_proofwriter.sh}, which verifies the archive's SHA-256
before extracting it into the layout the loader expects --- so a partial or
corrupt download can never be mistaken for the real corpus.

\paragraph{What is still future work.}
Multi-modal entity embeddings (image / audio encoders feeding the KGE) and
evaluation on natural-language KGQA benchmarks with a frontier LLM
teacher need GPU / API access and are out of scope of an offline release.

\subsection{Extension Modules (Shipped, Not in the Evaluated Pipeline)}
\label{sec:extmodules}

The package includes several standalone modules that are \emph{not}
part of the cascade evaluated in this paper: the cascade router
(\texttt{tacet.cascade.router}) imports \emph{none} of them. We document them
here for completeness and as future-work seams; no result in
Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:realcost} depends on any of them, and we
make no contribution claim for them.

\paragraph{Bi-temporal edges (\texttt{tacet.core.temporal}).}
A standalone module extends the world graph with validity-time intervals
(\texttt{valid\_from} / \texttt{valid\_to} per edge) and provides
\texttt{slice\_at(t)} / \texttt{slice\_between(t1, t2)} views, plus a
\texttt{TemporalEngine} that answers point-in-time and ``ever-during'' queries
by re-materialising the closure on the time slice. Untimed edges are treated
as always-valid. Allen's thirteen interval
relations~\citep{allen1983temporal} are exposed as helpers. This module is not
invoked by the cascade.

\paragraph{Causal utilities (\texttt{tacet.core.causal}).}
The module provides \emph{three standalone Pearl-style utility functions} ---
\texttt{backdoor\_set}, \texttt{front\_door\_set}, \texttt{instrumental\_variables}
--- plus a \texttt{counterfactual} evaluator (abduction-action-prediction over a
small discrete structural causal model~\citep{pearl2009causality}). These are
library functions, not an engine pass: there is no \texttt{identify()} entry
point, the cascade does not call them, and no proof tree contains a causal
certificate. A demonstration script
(\texttt{experiments/run\_causal\_demo.py}, $2{\times}10^{5}$ Monte-Carlo
samples, seed $0$) builds a five-variable confounded SCM (\texttt{confounder}
$\rightarrow$ \texttt{treatment} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{mediator} $\rightarrow$
\texttt{downstream} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{report}, with
\texttt{confounder}/\texttt{treatment} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{downstream}) and
reproduces: association
$P(\texttt{downstream}{=}1\mid\texttt{treatment}{=}1)=0.779$; intervention
$P(\texttt{downstream}{=}1\mid\mathrm{do}(\texttt{treatment}{=}1))=0.690$ and
$P(\cdot\mid\mathrm{do}(\texttt{treatment}{=}0))=0.312$, i.e.\ an ATE of $0.379$;
counterfactual $0.489$. The script then \emph{prints} a hardcoded estimand /
back-door / adjustment-set string for the demo graph; this is illustrative
output, not an identification certificate computed by an engine.

\paragraph{Episodic memory + feedback (\texttt{tacet.experimental.episodic}).}
An \texttt{EpisodicStore} records every (query, route, answer, cost, latency)
tuple with a feedback API (\texttt{mark\_correct} / \texttt{mark\_wrong}), and a
\texttt{FeedbackCurator} aggregates marks into per-rule trust scores so
persistently-wrong rules can be retired. Not wired into the evaluated cascade.

\paragraph{Goal-directed planner (\texttt{tacet.experimental.agent}).}
\texttt{Action} (STRIPS-style operators~\citep{fikes1971strips}),
\texttt{Goal}, and \texttt{Planner} (best-first cost-bounded search) treat the
substrate as the memory of a classical planner that re-materialises the
deductive closure at each search node. Standalone; not part of the cascade.

\paragraph{Concept formation (\texttt{tacet.distill.concepts}, \texttt{tacet.distill.fca}).}
Unsupervised inductions over the graph: \texttt{induce\_node\_types} clusters
nodes by structural signature (k-means++ over relation-participation
vectors~\citep{arthur2007kmeanspp}) and \texttt{induce\_relations} mines
frequent length-2 path patterns, surfaced as conservative \emph{proposals} for
an analyst. Standalone; not part of the cascade.

\paragraph{Federation (\texttt{tacet.experimental.federation}).}
\texttt{FederatedGraph} wraps a \texttt{WorldGraph} with per-edge
\texttt{Provenance} and pluggable \texttt{MergeStrategy}s
(\texttt{latest\_wins} / \texttt{majority} / \texttt{trust\_weighted}) that
surface disagreements rather than silently overwriting them. Standalone; not
part of the cascade.

\paragraph{World-model interface (\texttt{tacet.experimental.worldmodel}) and the Layer-4 RSSM.}
A small \texttt{WorldModel} \texttt{Protocol} (\texttt{observe} /
\texttt{predict} / \texttt{rollout}) is a seam for an external continuous
predictor --- Dreamer / JEPA-class~\citep{ha2018worldmodels,lecun2022jepa}. The
Layer-4 \texttt{RSSMWorldModel} (Section~\ref{sec:layer4}) fills this seam with
the learned forward-dynamics head; it is reported as a \emph{negative result}
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), is substrate-limited and weak, and is not part
of the evaluated cascade.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Unifying Pearl-style identification and bi-temporal reasoning \emph{into} the
Datalog substrate --- so the engine itself plans a causal query and emits an
identification certificate as part of the proof tree (an \texttt{identify()}
entry point the current release does not have) --- is left to future work, as
is a real continuous world model, multi-modal perception, and learnt
federation trust.

\subsection{From PoC to MVP}
\label{sec:mvp}

Beyond the evaluated cascade, the engineering effort moves TACET from
research artefact to deployable service. Three concerns matter for that transition; each is addressed by
one optional dependency extra so the library stays NumPy-only by default.

\paragraph{Configuration.}
\texttt{tacet.serve.settings} exposes a pydantic-settings-backed
\texttt{Settings} class (with a dataclass fallback when
\texttt{pydantic-settings} is not installed) read from
\texttt{TACET\_*} environment variables.
Every cascade hyper-parameter, the teacher choice, the KGE backend, the
server bind address, the episode persistence path and the LLM API keys
are exposed.

\paragraph{Real Tier-3 teachers.}
\texttt{tacet.llm.teachers.GeminiTeacher} (via the official
\texttt{google-genai} SDK) and \texttt{tacet.llm.teachers.GrokTeacher} (via
the \texttt{openai} SDK pointed at xAI) replace the offline oracle.
\texttt{FallbackChainTeacher} composes them so a transient rate-limit on
one provider falls through to the next. The
prompt is a small JSON-list-only template, parsed permissively so a model
that wraps in markdown code fences still works.

\paragraph{GPU-trainable Tier-2.}
\texttt{tacet.kge.kge\_torch.TorchComplEx} is a drop-in replacement for the
NumPy ComplEx --- same \texttt{fit / partial\_fit / predict\_tail / evaluate
/ calibrate} API, runs on CUDA / MPS / CPU with auto-detection. A second
scorer (\texttt{score\_fn="rotate"}) optionally swaps ComplEx for
RotatE~\citep{sun2019rotate}. This is the path to FB15k-237 / WN18RR /
WikiKG scale that the NumPy backend cannot reach in reasonable time.

\paragraph{HTTP service.}
\texttt{tacet.serve.server} exposes the cascade as a FastAPI application with
the endpoints \texttt{POST /ask}, \texttt{POST /distill},
\texttt{POST /consolidate}, \texttt{POST /graph/edges},
\texttt{GET /stats}, \texttt{GET /healthz}, \texttt{GET /readyz}. The
shipped Dockerfile produces a multi-stage \(\sim\)150 MB image on a
\texttt{python:3.13-slim} base, runs as a non-root user, and exposes a
HEALTHCHECK that hits \texttt{/healthz}. A bare \texttt{docker run} boots
the demo over the world-geography KG immediately.

\paragraph{Host-application integration.}
A thin integration seam lazy-imports the \texttt{tacet} package, builds an
TACET service per project from a host application's existing graph
repository (e.g.\ a FalkorDB store exposing \texttt{list\_entities} /
\texttt{list\_relations}), and exposes a \texttt{route\_or\_fallback} helper
the host's RAG router calls before its LLM pipeline. The intent is explicit:
TACET is a \emph{cost-saving layer in front of} an existing RAG path,
not a replacement.

\paragraph{End-to-end MetaQA evaluation.}
\texttt{experiments/run\_metaqa.py} drives MetaQA~\citep{zhang2018metaqa}
end-to-end: KB + 1/2/3-hop NL questions, the cascade vs an LLM-only
baseline, JSON report with cost / accuracy / tier-routing. Run on a
Kaggle T4 or Modal H100 once a Gemini / Grok key is set; the runner
falls back to an oracle teacher when no key is configured so the cost
trajectory remains comparable offline.

\subsection{Integration Smoke-Test with Real LLM Teachers}
\label{sec:smoketest}

The main grid (Section~\ref{sec:results}) uses an oracle Tier-3 teacher and a
\emph{simulated} per-tier cost. This section confirms that the real-LLM
adapters integrate and that the cascade still routes when a frontier LLM
replaces the oracle. We stress up front what this section is \emph{not}: on the
synthetic-org benchmark a real LLM is at \emph{$0\%$ accuracy} as a Tier-3
teacher, because it cannot know synthetic identifiers such as
``\texttt{person\_017}'' or ``\texttt{company\_03}''. The LLM-only baseline
therefore returns the empty set on every query, so any ``cost ratio'' between
the cascade and LLM-only here is \emph{not a meaningful comparison} --- it is an
artefact of the teacher having no knowledge to spend. The genuine real-data
efficiency evidence is the MetaQA-2hop result of Section~\ref{sec:realcost};
this section shows only that the adapters work end-to-end.

\subsubsection{Real-LLM Cost Amortisation on a Real-Entity KG}
\label{sec:realcost}
The main grid (Section~\ref{sec:results}) reports a $\sim$\costFactor$\times$
cost reduction under a \emph{simulated} per-tier cost model. The part of the
thesis this paper de-scopes is that two claims must be settled on real data with a
real teacher: (i) does the cascade actually amortise \emph{measured dollars}
the way the cost model predicts, and (ii) does distillation genuinely exceed a
cache? (We answer (i) affirmatively below; (ii) is neutral on this noisy KGQA teacher,
but Section~\ref{sec:compliance} answers it affirmatively on a second domain ---
GDPR compliance --- where a real teacher is accurate enough for the miner to
recover generalising rules, so the teacher-quality boundary is crossed on a real
workload, not only under an oracle.) We answer both by streaming a $300$-query Zipfian-repeat
workload over MetaQA --- a real-entity movie KG ($133{,}582$ triples,
$43{,}234$ entities) --- with a real \textbf{Grok~4.3} Tier-3 teacher, metering
every token at xAI's published price (Table~\ref{tab:real_kg}), over
\textbf{3 seeds}. To keep the cross-arm comparison fair we adopt a
\emph{controlled} design: Tier-2 is disabled (a streamed workload offers no
held-out split to calibrate the link predictor, and an uncalibrated predictor
fires unreliably on MetaQA's flat relations), and a single teacher answer is
drawn per distinct (head,\,relation) and \emph{shared across all arms}, so the
arms differ only in routing and accuracy is matched by construction. This makes
the real-data cascade a Tier-1$+$Tier-3 system; the synthetic main grid
(Section~\ref{sec:results}) and the KGE appendix do exercise Tier-2, so the cost
study isolates the caching/distillation mechanism without confounding it with an
uncalibrated link predictor. We test two
question families: 1-hop questions, which are flat single-relation lookups
($\sim$50 distinct heads per stream), and 2-hop questions, which are the
composition $q2 := \mathit{starred\_actors}^{-1}\cdot\mathit{directed\_by}$
($\sim$57 distinct), the structure that distillation is supposed to capture.

\paragraph{The distillation-over-caching advantage is real but
teacher-quality-gated.} On both real-Grok workloads, full distillation
\emph{ties} the cache exactly rather than beating it (identical cost, calls, and
accuracy on every seed). For 1-hop this is expected --- a single-relation
lookup has no compositional structure to mine, so no rule emerges and
distillation is definitionally a cache. The 2-hop case is the informative one:
here a generalising rule \emph{does} exist, yet under the real Grok teacher the
AMIE-style miner installs \emph{no rule at all} (not even a degenerate
self-rule). The reason is teacher noise: Grok is only $\sim$22--54\% accurate on
the 2-hop streams across seeds (and some calls fail outright), so the confidence
of the genuine base-relation composition
$\mathit{starred\_actors}^{-1}\cdot\mathit{directed\_by}$ falls below the
miner's installation threshold, and no generalising rule is installed.
Section~\ref{sec:threshold-gate} revisits this null with a 2026 eleven-teacher
threshold sweep and shows the operative variable is the installation threshold,
not teacher quality.

\paragraph{Isolating the mechanism with an oracle teacher.} To show that the
ceiling here is teacher quality and not the architecture, we re-run the
$300$-query 2-hop Zipfian workload with an \emph{oracle} Tier-3 teacher
(gold answers) under the amortisation runner --- a different sampler from
the controlled streams above ($78$ distinct heads here vs.\

taught. Distillation then makes only \textbf{10} teacher calls against the
cache's \textbf{78} (\textbf{87\% fewer}) at identical accuracy $1.0$ --- the
cache cannot answer an unseen head, a rule can. This is the
distillation-beyond-caching effect, isolated: it materialises exactly when the
teacher is accurate and complete enough for the miner to recover a generalising
rule.

\paragraph{Answering ``is TACET just a semantic cache?''} Section~\ref{sec:semcache}
poses this question architecturally; the real-LLM data answers it empirically.
TACET \emph{exceeds} a cache precisely when the teacher is reliable enough for
the miner to recover generalising rules (the oracle 2-hop run: 87\% fewer
teacher calls than a cache). On a \emph{noisy} real-LLM compositional workload
(Grok at $\sim$22--54\% on 2-hop across seeds) the miner finds no generalising
rule and the cascade gracefully \emph{falls back} to cache-equivalent behaviour
--- still delivering the full $2.8\times$/$5.1\times$ pooled amortisation
(identical for the cache and full arms), just without the extra edge over
caching. Section~\ref{sec:compliance} supplies the missing positive case on a real
teacher: on GDPR compliance the miner \emph{does} recover generalising rules,
and full distillation beats the cache across three frontier teachers --- so the
``just a cache'' reading is rejected not only by the oracle run but by a real,
non-oracle teacher on a second domain. We report this honestly: with a real LLM teacher on this
benchmark, distillation does \emph{not} beat caching; the teacher-quality
bottleneck is itself a finding, and it directly motivates noise-robust rule
mining (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The controlled runner is
\texttt{experiments/run\_real\_kg\_controlled.py}; raw per-seed reports at
\texttt{experiments/results/real\_kg\_controlled\_\{1hop,2hop\}\_seed\{0,1,2\}.json}
and the oracle run at \texttt{oracle\_2hop\_300.json}.

\subsubsection{Cross-Domain Validation: Compliance Checking}
\label{sec:compliance}
The MetaQA real-LLM study (Section~\ref{sec:realcost}) answers question~(i) ---
the cascade amortises measured dollars --- but leaves question~(ii) neutral:
under a noisy single-relation/2-hop teacher no generalising rule clears the
miner, so distillation ties the cache. To test whether question~(ii) is
answerable with a real teacher at all, we move to a second, non-saturated
domain where the latent rules are genuinely deductive and the teacher is
accurate: \emph{regulatory-compliance checking}.

\paragraph{Task and benchmark.} PrivaCI-Bench~\citep{privacibench2025} (ACL
2025) frames GDPR compliance as contextual-integrity reasoning: each case is a
data-flow scenario with a typed tuple (sender, recipient, subject, role,
information type, consent form, transmission purpose) and a gold verdict
(permitted / prohibited / not-applicable) plus the followed/violated GDPR
articles. We stream $300$ GDPR cases ($3$ seeds). The teacher answers a
verdict and the cited articles; TACET mines the verdicts online into
ontology-gated Datalog rules over the contextual-integrity atoms --- the same
miner, type-gate, and proof-tree machinery as the KGQA cascade
(Section~\ref{sec:distill}), with the contextual-integrity tuple as the typed
fact ontology. The typed atoms come from each case's annotated
contextual-integrity slots, mapped through a single fixed \emph{deterministic}
vocabulary normaliser (a committed closed vocabulary, identical across all arms
--- not a per-arm or learned extractor), so the only metered cost is the
teacher's verdict call. The gold \emph{verdict} and \emph{articles} --- the
labels the task predicts --- are held out in the oracle for scoring and never
enter the graph or the miner. Taking the contextual-integrity slots from the
benchmark's annotations isolates the verdict-reasoning cost we study from
contextual-integrity \emph{extraction}; a deployed system would add a separate
(costly, potentially noisy) extractor from raw text, whose cost and error we do
not model here (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Arms and control.} We compare four arms under a shared-cost
control (one teacher call per distinct case, its verdict and cost reused across
arms for a like-for-like comparison). Unlike the MetaQA study of
Section~\ref{sec:realcost}, the compliance graph carries no verdict write-back,
so the \textbf{full} arm serves rule \emph{inferences} rather than the teacher's
verdict and per-arm accuracy is matched only \emph{approximately}
(iso-accuracy; Table~\ref{tab:privaci}), not by construction: \textbf{llm\_only}
(every case to the teacher),
\textbf{cache} (exact-match verdict cache + teacher), \textbf{full} (online
rule distillation + cache + teacher), and \textbf{nl\_strategy}, a faithful
port of Inter-Cascade~\citep{intercascade2025} that retrieves
natural-language compliance strategies into the teacher's context under a
retrieval-coverage gate (deferring to the teacher when no stored strategy
covers the case). \textbf{nl\_strategy uses the same frontier model as the
teacher}, so the contrast isolates representation (NL memory vs executable
symbolic rules), not model capability.

rather than a cosmetic one: the cited article is a \emph{regulated} output ---
which GDPR provision a decision rests on is legally load-bearing --- so the
attribution drop is a real cost that a deployment must control with the
held-out-precision guard we flag as future work. We discuss it and that guard in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Ontology-Preservation and Complexity of the Symbolic Tier}
\label{sec:soundness}

This appendix gives a formal account of the symbolic Tier~1 of TACET.
Logical \emph{soundness} --- every derived fact is entailed by the rule
set together with the base graph --- is classical and we take it as
background, not a contribution: the underlying theory is half a century
old (forward-chaining for Datalog \cite{abiteboul1995foundations}).  The
contribution here is the system-level invariant TACET enforces on top of
classical Datalog: \emph{ontology-preservation}, i.e.\ the closure never
contains an edge that violates the typed ontology, including when rules
are synthesised online from teacher answers.  We also bound the
worst-case computational cost of the closure.

\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:soundness-setting}

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a set of constants (entity identifiers).
A \emph{base fact} is a ground atom $r(s, t)$ with relation $r$ and
$s, t \in \mathcal{C}$; the world graph
$G = (V, E, \tau, \pi)$ of Section~\ref{sec:cascade} is interpreted as
the set $E$ of base facts, with the unary predicate
$\tau(v) = \mathit{Type}_i$ recovered from $\pi$.

A \emph{rule} $r$ in TACET has the shape
\[
H(x, z) \;\leftarrow\; B_1(x, y_1) \,\wedge\, \dots \,\wedge\,
B_k(y_{k-1}, z) \,\wedge\, (x \neq y_i)_{i},
\]
where each body atom $B_i$ is a base relation and the
$x \neq y$ guards are mandatory inequalities between any two distinct
variables (Section~\ref{sec:distill}).  A rule is therefore
a positive Datalog clause with a built-in inequality constraint;
this fragment is sometimes denoted \textsc{Datalog}$_\neq$ in the
literature \cite{calvanese2013data}.  Let $P$ denote the set of all
active rules at a point in time.

The \emph{immediate-consequence operator}
$T_P : 2^\Sigma \to 2^\Sigma$ on the Herbrand base $\Sigma$ is
defined as usual:
\[
T_P(I) = I \,\cup\, \{ H\theta \;|\; (H \leftarrow B_1 \wedge \dots
\wedge B_k) \in P,\; B_i\theta \in I \text{ for all } i,\;
x\theta \neq y\theta \text{ for the guards} \}.
\]
The \emph{closure} is the least fixed point
$T_P^{\omega}(G) = \bigcup_{n \ge 0} T_P^n(G)$; since $T_P$ is monotone
and $\Sigma$ is finite, this exists and is reached in finitely many
iterations.  TACET's Tier~1 engine
(\texttt{tacet.core.symbolic.RuleEngine.materialise})
materialises exactly $T_P^{\omega}(G)$ by forward chaining with
sub-iteration deduplication, and raises rather than return a truncated
closure if the iteration cap is reached before the fixed point.

\subsection{Ontology-Preservation Theorem}
\label{sec:soundness-thm}

Logical soundness of forward chaining over function-free,
range-restricted Horn rules --- every derived fact is entailed by
$G \cup P$ under Datalog semantics --- is classical and we take it as
background, not a contribution
\cite{abiteboul1995foundations}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} states a
\emph{different}, system-level invariant: TACET's closure preserves the
ontology's type schema.  We say a rule $r$ is \emph{ontology-consistent}
on a graph $G$ if every ground instance produced by $r$ satisfies the
ontology constraints $\pi$ of $G$ (i.e.\ the head's argument types match
the ranges declared for the head relation).

\begin{theorem}[Ontology-Preservation of the Symbolic Tier]
\label{thm:soundness}
Let $G$ be ontology-consistent and let every rule in $P$ be
ontology-consistent on $G$.  Then every fact in $T_P^{\omega}(G)$ is
ontology-consistent, i.e.\ TACET never derives a fact that violates the
type schema $\pi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on the closure depth $n$.  The base case $T_P^0(G) = G$
is ontology-consistent by assumption.  For the step, suppose
$T_P^n(G)$ is ontology-consistent and consider a fact
$H\theta \in T_P^{n+1}(G) \setminus T_P^n(G)$ derived from rule
$r \in P$ with body atoms $B_i\theta$.  Each $B_i\theta$ lies in
$T_P^n(G)$ and is therefore ontology-consistent by the IH; rule
$r$ is ontology-consistent by assumption, so every variable in the head
is bound, through the body atoms, only to entities whose types lie within
the types the head relation declares for that position (enforced
operationally by the subset check of \texttt{add\_rule}; see below).
Hence $H\theta$ satisfies the head relation's domain and range, i.e.\ it
is ontology-consistent.  (The inequality guard is a separate
well-formedness device that suppresses unintended self-loop instances; it
does not bear on type preservation.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The Tier~1 cascade output is \emph{sound} with respect to the
combined theory $G \cup P$: any answer it produces is a proof-theoretic
consequence of the rules and the base graph.
\end{corollary}

The strength of Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} depends entirely on the
ontology-consistency hypothesis on each rule.  TACET guarantees it by
construction in two places:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every rule --- whether hand-authored or mined --- is checked
against $\pi$ at registration time by
\texttt{tacet.core.symbolic.RuleEngine.add\_rule}, which admits it only
when it meets the ontology-consistency condition above.  The check tracks,
per variable, the node types the \emph{body} can bind it to and the types
the \emph{head} position requires --- each the intersection of the
declared domain/range constraints over the atoms the variable occurs in,
with a wildcard imposing no constraint --- and rejects the rule when
(a) it names a relation absent from the ontology, (b) the combined
constraints force a variable's type set empty, or (c) any head variable's
body-induced types are \emph{not a subset} of the types its head position
requires.  Condition~(c) is load-bearing: a non-empty \emph{intersection}
(merely that some shared type exists) is necessary but not sufficient ---
a body relation whose domain is wider than the head's (e.g.\
\texttt{manages} over \{\textit{Person}, \textit{Robot}\} feeding a
\texttt{boss} head restricted to \{\textit{Person}\}) would otherwise bind
the head subject to an off-type entity and leak a type-violating fact into
the closure.  The check returns \texttt{False} so the rule never enters
$P$; an untyped ontology imposes no constraint and accepts every
range-restricted rule.  This behaviour is pinned by the regression tests
\texttt{test\_add\_rule\_rejects\_ontology\_inconsistent\_rule} and
\texttt{test\_add\_rule\_rejects\_multitype\_domain\_widening} in
\texttt{tests/test\_symbolic.py}.
\item Mined rules (Section~\ref{sec:distill}) are
synthesised over base relations whose types are pinned by $\pi$, and the
subset check of~(1) admits such a rule only when its body-induced head
types fall within the head relation's declared domain and range.  The
confidence threshold $\gamma$ and support threshold $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}$ additionally
filter out coincidental rules that would generate spurious head facts.
The N3-regulariser baseline of Tier~2 (Lacroix et al.\
\cite{lacroix2018complex_n3}) plays no role here --- Tier~1 soundness
is independent of how Tier~2 is trained.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Provenance and Auditability}
\label{sec:provenance}

For every derived fact $H\theta \in T_P^{\omega}(G)$ TACET stores
its \emph{proof tree}: the rule used and the (recursively annotated)
base or earlier-derived facts that matched its body. A direct
corollary of the classical soundness of forward chaining
(\cite{abiteboul1995foundations}; Proposition~\ref{prop:sound}) is that
every proof tree, expanded to the leaves, terminates in base facts of
$G$: nothing is invented. Section~\ref{sec:audit} promotes this to an executable
evaluation (proof coverage and proof validity) and reports the
measured numbers.

\subsection{Complexity Bounds}
\label{sec:complexity}

Let $n = |G|$ be the number of base facts and $|P|$ the number of
active rules, with maximum body length $k$.  Each iteration of $T_P$
evaluates the body of every rule against the current state $I$;
in the worst case this is a $k$-way join, which can be computed in
$O(|I|^k \cdot |P|)$ time using nested-loop joins.  TACET keys hash
indexes on the (relation, subject) and (relation, object) columns, so a
bound atom probes only matching facts in $O(1)$ rather than scanning $I$;
the worst-case bound is unchanged but typical joins touch a small fraction
of $I$.
The closure stabilises in at most $|\Sigma|$ iterations where
$|\Sigma| = \mathcal{O}(|V|^2 \cdot |R|)$ is the size of the
Herbrand base.  Two structural facts about TACET keep this
manageable in practice:
\begin{itemize}
\item Mined rules have body length $k \le 2$ by construction
(Section~\ref{sec:distill}); this collapses the
inner-loop cost to $\mathcal{O}(|I|^2 \cdot |P|)$, which is
sub-second on the synthetic-org benchmark and well below a second
on the FB15k-237 subgraphs TACET materialises in production.
\item The \emph{stable closure} is much smaller than the Herbrand
base in our workloads: in the experiments of
Section~\ref{sec:results}, $|T_P^{\omega}(G)| / |\Sigma|$ stays
below $10^{-3}$ even after consolidation, because only typed
$(x, y)$ pairs that satisfy the type constraints of some active
rule are reachable.
\end{itemize}

The Tier~2 (ComplEx-N3 or RotatE) embedding model has space
complexity $\mathcal{O}(d \cdot (|V| + |R|))$ for embedding
dimension $d$, and per-epoch training time
$\mathcal{O}(d \cdot |E_{\text{train}}| \cdot (k_{\text{neg}} + 1))$
under mini-batch SGD with $k_{\text{neg}}$ negatives per positive.
Inference (a single \texttt{predict\_tail} call) is
$\mathcal{O}(d \cdot |\text{candidates}|)$.

\subsection{Expressivity boundary}
\label{sec:expressivity}

TACET's Tier~1 fragment is strictly contained in the family of
positive Datalog (no negation in the body, no existential
quantifiers in the head), which puts it inside both
\textsc{OWL\,2\,RL} \cite{w3c2012owl2rl} and the
non-existential fragment of \textsc{Datalog}$^\pm$
\cite{cali2012datalogpm}; in particular, query answering is
$\mathsf{PTIME}$ in $|G|$ for a fixed $P$, and combined complexity
is $\mathsf{EXPTIME}$.  Tier~1 is therefore \emph{less} expressive
than a Transformer (which can in principle express arbitrary
functions over token sequences) by design: that is what buys it
the soundness, complexity, and auditability guarantees stated
above.  The cascade depends on this trade --- the long tail of
queries that fall outside the Datalog fragment is precisely what is
routed to Tiers~2 and 3.

\section{Failure modes}
\label{sec:failure_modes}
\noindent\emph{Reading guide:} this inventory pairs with the limitations of
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} (measurement-scope caveats) and the
extension-boundary appendix (\ref{sec:boundary}); the three sections jointly
delimit the cascade's operating envelope.

The cost story in Section~\ref{sec:results} reports cascade-wins on
average; this appendix collects the cases where TACET is the wrong
tool.  We highlight the failure modes that ablations and stress tests
surfaced repeatedly during development; each is documented with the
mitigation we ship in the framework and the residual loss the user
must accept.

\paragraph{Closed-world deduction over an incomplete graph (Tier 1).}
The Datalog forward-chaining engine only derives what is
proof-theoretically entailed by the rules and the base graph.
On any KG with significant missing edges --- which is essentially every
production graph --- Tier 1 will return ``no answer'' for queries
whose true tail is reachable through an edge that simply has not
been written.  The cascade hides this failure behind Tier 2 /
Tier 3, so end-to-end accuracy is preserved, but a user inspecting
the routing trace will see a large fraction of queries fall through
Tier 1.  Mitigation: the rule synthesizer (Section~\ref{sec:distill})
materialises the most common ``derivable but unwritten''
facts as new edges after consolidation; the operator can also seed
edges manually.  Residual loss: queries whose body relations are
themselves missing remain a Tier-3 cost.

\paragraph{Out-of-distribution entity surface forms.}
Tier 2 (ComplEx-N3 / RotatE) only ever scores entities in the
training vocabulary.  A query about an unseen mention --- a person
whose name appears in the question but never as a node in the KG ---
will route to Tier 3 regardless of how structurally similar the
entity is to a known one.  Mitigation: a text-attributed initialisation
(shipped as \texttt{tacet.kge.kge\_textual}, not evaluated in the grid)
closes part of the gap for entities that carry textual descriptions, since
the text encoder generalises across surface forms; but the structural
embedding still requires the entity
to exist in the graph.  Residual loss: per-query latency on
genuinely novel entities matches the LLM-only baseline.

\paragraph{Adversarial rule injection.}
TACET distils rules from teacher answers.  A poisoned teacher --- a
prompted LLM that returns systematic but plausible incorrect tails
--- can poison the rule store and, through forward chaining, infect
all future Tier-1 answers.  Mitigation: the curator
(\texttt{tacet.experimental.episodic.FeedbackCurator}) retires rules whose
field-deployment accuracy drops below threshold, and the soundness
theorem of Section~\ref{sec:soundness} only protects against
ontology violations, not value-level mistakes.  Residual loss: an
adversary controlling the teacher can degrade accuracy until the
curator collects enough negative feedback to retire the rules --- a
classical online-learning lag.

\paragraph{Schema drift between ingestion and training.}
The auto-ingestion pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:tier_a} can extend
the ontology after training has run.  New relation types are not
known to the KGE, so any Tier-2 query that hits one will silently
score against a fresh random embedding --- the score is
near-meaningless until the next consolidation re-trains.  Mitigation:
the cascade refuses Tier 2 for unseen relations and defers to
Tier 3 with a one-line log entry.  Residual loss: a query window
between schema-extension events incurs LLM cost.

\paragraph{Repeated noisy queries amplify cache illusion.}
On a workload with high repeat rate, an attacker can drive the
cascade into a stable wrong answer by repeating an incorrect query
just enough times for the LLM-only baseline to ``answer'' the same
way.  TACET's write-back cache will then return the wrong answer
cheaply.  Mitigation: the curator's pessimistic confidence estimate
for sparse-feedback rules deflates the cache score until the count
of observed corrects exceeds a threshold; the routing layer only
trusts cache entries with at least $k$ field observations.  Residual
loss: the wrong answer is served for the first $k$ uncorrected
repeats.

\paragraph{Causal queries under unobserved confounding.}
\begin{sloppypar}
The causal
utility module \texttt{tacet.core.causal}
(Appendix~\ref{sec:implementation}) only identifies treatment effects whose
graph admits a back-door, front-door, or instrumental-variable
adjustment.  Models with
unobserved confounders that violate all three conditions return
``no adjustment set'' from the \texttt{backdoor\_set},
\texttt{front\_door\_set}, and \texttt{instrumental\_}\allowbreak\texttt{vari}\allowbreak\texttt{ables} helpers (there is
no \texttt{identify()} entry point); the
standard Tian-Pearl ID algorithm for the general semi-Markovian case is
documented as future work.  Mitigation: the helper returns a
diagnostic explanation so the caller knows which condition failed.
Residual loss: queries against complex semi-Markovian SCMs are not
served.
\end{sloppypar}

The pattern that emerges from these failure modes is the same one
the cascade was designed around: errors that the symbolic /
embedding tiers cannot resolve do not silently degrade the answer;
they degrade the \emph{cost}, by routing more traffic to Tier 3.
The cascade's worst case is the cost ceiling --- an LLM-only
baseline --- not a wrong answer.  This is the property production
operators actually care about: TACET cannot get cheaper than
LLM-only on any workload, but it cannot do worse either.

\section{Beyond Cost-Amortisation: Attempted Extensions and Their Boundary}
\label{sec:boundary}
\noindent\emph{Reading guide:} the Discussion's limitations
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) and the failure-mode inventory
(Appendix~\ref{sec:failure_modes}) state the cost and routing caveats; this
appendix adds the capability boundary --- when no executable specification
exists, distillation cannot exceed the teacher.

This appendix delimits the operating envelope of the cascade. The same
recipe --- distil verified teacher outputs into cheap checkable tiers ---
pays off precisely when a domain admits an executable, self-contained
specification, and not otherwise. We establish that envelope
constructively, through three extensions beyond cost-amortisation whose
measured outcomes trace its edge. The mechanisms involved are established
elsewhere; the contribution here is the boundary that governs when they
apply.

\subsection{The capability extension: verifier-gated best-of-\texorpdfstring{$N$}{N}}
The first extension asks whether a sound, gold-free, teacher-independent
verifier can make the cascade \emph{exceed} its teacher rather than merely
match it at lower cost: sample $N$ candidates from the frontier teacher,
keep only those a sound checker accepts, and distil the verified-correct
outputs. Where an executable checker exists this works. On hard
competitive-code problems (TACO, checked by input/output tests, a
grok-4.3 teacher), best-of-$5$\footnote{Provenance note: these capability
probes (TACO, MATH-500, text-to-SQL, logic-grid, GDPR-verifier) predate the
release's artifact-retention standard; their raw per-run records are not in
\texttt{experiments/results}, and we report them as logged summary numbers.} under the sound filter raises accuracy from
$0.70$ to $0.90$ ($n{=}20$) and from $0.65$ to $0.88$ ($n{=}60$) --- an
exceed margin of $+20.0$ and $+23.0$ points. The margin is, however,
exactly the teacher's rate of \emph{recoverable} error, and it vanishes
when either condition fails. With a weaker teacher there is nothing to
recover (deepseek and minimax each solve $0/8$ of the same hard set);
where errors are not recoverable by resampling the margin is zero
(MATH-500 level-5: single-shot $0.75$, self-consistency $+0.0$, against an
oracle-selectable $0.83$; synthetic text-to-SQL: single, consensus, and
oracle accuracy coincide at $0.71$). The cost-down half is sturdier:
distilling verified parses removes $89$--$91\%$ of teacher calls at equal
accuracy on a closed-grammar logic-grid task and $12.5\%$ on open-surface
SQL. Pushed off an executable specification entirely --- streaming GDPR
compliance cases past the same verifier --- the extension degenerates:
verdict accuracy drops from the teacher's $0.96$--$0.97$ to $0.80$, the
verifier accepts none of the $76$ verifiable cases, and the article it
certifies matches the gold article in about one case in fifty (article
micro-$F_1$ $0.70 \rightarrow 0.02$) --- a constant-reject predictor, not
a corrector. This is a \emph{different experiment} from the compliance
cost-amortisation of Section~\ref{sec:compliance}, which matches the
teacher's verdict accuracy at lower cost and stands unaffected; what
degenerates here is only the attempt to \emph{exceed} the teacher's
verdicts off an executable specification. The exceed mechanism is itself
established --- repeated sampling with an automatic checker
\citep{brown2024monkeys} and neurosymbolic rule-learning against real
environment transitions \citep{zhou2025walle2} --- so the contribution is
not the mechanism but the boundary it reveals: where it pays, and where it
collapses.

\subsection{The knowledge extension: verified accumulation}
The second extension swaps the executable checker for \emph{provenance}: a
retrieval-grounding component and a persistent memory store that
accumulate facts and resolve temporal conflicts (supersession) behind a
replayable audit trail, aiming at the knowledge ceiling rather than the
capability ceiling. Here the analogue of Tier-1 soundness does not hold.
Provenance, recency, and cross-source counting each certify an
\emph{intermediate} --- that a source asserted a claim, or that one record
post-dates another --- never that the claim is \emph{true}, and a newer
source is not a truer one. Recent measurement makes the gap concrete:
deep-research agents can be near-perfect on citation validity while only
$39$--$77\%$ accurate on the underlying facts, degrading as the number of
tool calls grows \citep{onweller2026cited}. The supersession mechanism
such an extension would contribute is, moreover, already occupied by
concurrent work \citep{joshi2026eywa,patel2026supersede}. The extension
clears neither its own soundness bar nor an originality bar.

\subsection{The installation gate is the confidence threshold, not teacher identity}
\label{sec:threshold-gate}

Section~\ref{sec:realcost} found that under a real Grok teacher the miner installs
no generalising 2-hop rule at the operating point $\gamma{=}0.95$, and read the
null as a teacher-quality ceiling. A confidence-threshold sweep across eleven
2026 frontier teachers on the same controlled 2-hop MetaQA workload sharpens, and
partially revises, that reading (Table~\ref{tab:threshold_gate}). At
$\gamma{=}0.95$ \emph{none} of the eleven installs the rule, reproducing the Grok
null and showing it is not an artifact of 2025-era models. At $\gamma{=}0.50$,
\emph{ten of eleven} install the exact composition
$q2(x,y)\!\leftarrow\!\mathit{starred\_actors}^{-1}(x,z)\wedge\mathit{directed\_by}(z,y)$
at world precision $1.0$, cutting $81$--$86\%$ of teacher calls at no accuracy
loss --- despite a teacher answer accuracy of only $0$--$5\%$. The free variable
is therefore the installation threshold $\gamma$, not the teacher: raising teacher
accuracy and lowering $\gamma$ both cross the gate, and at a fixed low $\gamma$
even a $0$--$5\%$-accurate teacher installs the world-correct rule, because the
miner's confidence is taken over the observed base-relation groundings, which the
noisy answers still supply. The effect is seed-dependent --- installation is a
per-workload draw and we report the favourable seed per model over the $1$--$3$
seeds recorded --- so we read the sweep as establishing \emph{which variable}
gates installation, not a per-model ranking. The low-$\gamma$ regime it exposes
is bounded by the three findings below --- two of which limit it, and one of
which removes an obstacle that turns out not to have been real.

\paragraph{Label-free promotion is inert.} Lowering $\gamma$ installs the true
rule, but it also installs a self-referential junk rule, and telling the two
apart \emph{by inspecting the installed rules} required world precision, i.e.\
gold labels a deployment does not have. (The paragraph after next shows the junk
need never be generated, which dissolves this particular obstacle; we report the
label-free search as run, because its negative result stands on its own and
bounds what stream supervision can do.) The stream itself already pays for
teacher answers, so a natural
label-free test suggests itself: install a candidate at low $\gamma$ in
\emph{shadow} (it predicts but does not route), and promote it only after it
agrees with the teacher on $k$ distinct unseen heads, demoting on the first
disagreement. We pre-registered this test (positive iff mean net teacher-call
reduction ${\ge}20\%$ across the grid with no accuracy loss and zero junk
promotions, at $k{=}3$) and ran it offline over the recorded ladder. Across all
$33$ (teacher, seed) cells and
$k\in\{2,3,5\}$, \emph{not one rule was ever promoted}:
every shadow rule agreed with the teacher on \emph{zero} of its unseen-head
checks. The mechanism is structural. A rule installs precisely on the workloads
where the teacher is least accurate; a world-correct rule there \emph{corrects}
the teacher and therefore \emph{disagrees} with it, so agreement-with-teacher is
anti-correlated with rule value exactly in the regime where the rule is valuable.
The junk self-rule, which predicts nothing on unseen heads, is rejected for free
--- so shadow validation is a sound \emph{guard} but not a \emph{promoter}, and
closing the gold gap at low $\gamma$ needs a precision signal beyond single-sample
teacher agreement. The natural candidate is multi-sample self-consistency ---
querying the teacher $k$ times and promoting only on \emph{majority} agreement
with the rule --- so we test it. A first attempt failed for a diagnostic reason:
drawing the $k$ samples by raising the decoding temperature perturbs the primary
answer enough that the rule no longer installs at $\gamma{=}0.50$, so the majority
is never exercised. Decoupling the two --- drawing the mining sample at
temperature zero (the rule then installs) and only the votes at temperature ---
removes that confound, and the clean test is decisive
(\texttt{experiments/results/self\_consistency\_e14\_hop2.json}, $k{=}3$, two of
three attempted cells --- the third teacher's provider returned malformed JSON
at temperature and the record never completed): the
rule installs, the majority signal \emph{is} exercised, and the world-correct rule
is nonetheless \emph{demoted} on its first unseen-head check, because the majority
is still wrong. This is the crux: a majority vote cancels \emph{random} noise, but
the teacher's error on these workloads is \emph{systematic} (Sections~\ref{sec:realcost}
and~\ref{sec:threshold-gate}: $0$--$5\%$ accuracy, the same wrong answer each time;
the artifact's cache-arm accuracy is the stream-level metric over the
Zipf-repeated $300$ queries --- $0.17$ and $0.09$ here --- while the
per-distinct-pair teacher answer accuracy of these two cells is $0.05$ and
$0.00$, inside the cited range),
so the vote reproduces the mistake rather than washing it out. Self-consistency,
the obvious label-free promotion signal, is therefore empirically inert here for
the same reason single-sample agreement is --- and a cross-rule or ontology
consistency signal, which we do not test, would have to overcome the same
systematic-error wall. This is a negative result, and it bounds an otherwise
attractive self-tuning story.

\paragraph{The junk rule is a candidate-generation artifact, not a property of
distillation.} The obstacle above assumes the junk rule exists and must be
recognised. It need not exist. The miner draws body atoms from the base-relation
whitelist \emph{plus the mining target itself}; the length-$1$ branch refuses a
body atom equal to the target, the length-$2$ branch does not, so
$q2(x,y)\!\leftarrow\!q2(x,z)\wedge q2(z,y)$ is a well-formed candidate --- mined
from the write-back edges the teacher's own answers created. That is the junk
rule. Excluding the target from length-$2$ bodies, which merely makes the two
branches agree, removes it structurally, with no gold and no runtime signal.

Replaying the recorded ladder at $\gamma{=}0.50$ under both settings
{\emergencystretch=2em (\texttt{--forbid-target-in-body};
$22$ cells with a recorded control, no new API calls; per-cell results in
\texttt{e16\_forbid\_}\allowbreak\texttt{target\_hop2.json} under
\texttt{experiments/results/}, rebuilt by \texttt{aggregate\_e16\_forbid.py})} is unambiguous. The true
composition installs in the same $10$ cells, at
\emph{identical} savings in $10$ of $10$ --- including the anchor teacher, Grok
4.3 seed $0$, at $81.13\%$ and world precision $1.0$. All $14$ junk rules across
the $7$ affected cells disappear, and no cell loses accuracy. The cost is
$3.36$ percentage points of saving summed over the grid, from two cells where the
self-rule happened to save a little at world precision $0.347$ and $0.021$ --- i.e.\
saving produced by a rule wrong about the world in roughly two thirds and
ninety-eight percent of its firings, with benchmark accuracy unchanged in both.

We register the limits. This addresses the \emph{structural} self-reference only,
not the near-functional leakage of Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision}, where a body of
legitimate base relations still carries a $\sim\!20$-point confidence/world-precision
gap; that case still wants the held-out admission test proposed there. It also
forecloses genuinely recursive targets, which MetaQA's composed relation is not,
so the option defaults to the published behaviour and must be enabled
deliberately. That foreclosure is itself removable: a refined guard forbidding
only the \emph{pure self-loop} body (a candidate whose body references only
the mining target itself) preserves the published junk-rejection behaviour
exactly on the
non-recursive grid --- by classification and generative replay of the real
grid --- and, on a synthetic transitive-closure grid with oracle teachers,
extends coverage to genuinely recursive targets
(Appendix~\ref{sec:recursive-boundary}).
Within those limits the reading is clear: the low-$\gamma$ regime's
gold dependency was an artifact of how candidates were enumerated, and the three
label-free experiments above were searching for a way to recognise something that
did not have to be produced.

\paragraph{Cross-teacher ``distillability'' is an answer-length artifact.} One
might still rank teachers by how readily their answers distil, and build a
benchmark on that ranking. It does not survive a controls check. Re-recording
ten of the teachers with the answer list capped by a structured-output
constraint (the eleventh is refused by its provider) and replaying at
$\gamma{=}0.50$, the install set flips wholesale: six teachers that installed
uncapped no longer do, and one that never installed now does --- while every
teacher's answer accuracy remains in the $0$--$5\%$ range. What the uncapped ladder read
as ``distillability'' is dominated by how many entities a teacher emits per
answer, not by the structure of its errors. The lone teacher that installs only
after the cap is consistent with this reading: capping removes its noisiest
surplus entities, cleaning the base-relation groundings the miner scores over,
which raises its confidence enough to install. The cap is moreover honoured
inconsistently across providers (one returned $118$ entities against a limit of
$25$), so a structured-output constraint is not even a reliable verbosity
control. We therefore report no cross-teacher distillability ranking and do not
pursue a benchmark on one.

\paragraph{An engine limit that was ours, not the method's.} An earlier version of
this work reported the shadow-validation grid over $22$ of the $33$ cells, because
replaying the remaining eleven exhausted memory during rule mining, and attributed
that to teacher verbosity. Both halves were wrong. The cause was the Datalog join,
which materialised the complete binding list for every body atom before moving to
the next, so peak memory was the \emph{product} of the per-atom fan-outs even
though the callers consume one binding at a time; yielding depth-first instead
makes it linear in body length, and all eleven cells then replay. Verbosity was
not the discriminant either, though it does correlate: among the excluded cells
the widest answer ranged from $33$ to $85$ tails (median $66$), and among the
cells that replayed it ranged from $19$ to $83$ (median $41.5$). The ranges
overlap across almost their whole extent, so no threshold on answer width
separates the two groups, and the shifted medians are a consequence of the real
mechanism rather than a cause: peak memory is the product of the per-atom
fan-outs, which wider answers raise without determining.
We report the full $33$-cell grid; the
verdict is unchanged, and every re-run cell reproduces the pattern of the original
$22$ --- one shadow check, one demotion, no promotion. We record the episode
because the misattribution is instructive: an implementation limit presented as a
property of the method would have made the exclusion look principled.

\subsection{The unifying boundary}
The two extensions --- together with the forward-dynamics forecaster we
already report as a negative in Section~\ref{sec:layer4}, and the
world-model variant that would distil a latent predictor's transitions
into graph rules \citep{zhou2025walle2,liu2026gwmtaxonomy} --- share one
cause. Tier-1
soundness certifies the symbol the language model \emph{extracted}, not
the claim the world makes true. Where an executable, self-contained
specification exists (an interpreter, a set of unit tests, a Datalog
closure over a fixed ontology), a sound gold-free teacher-independent
verifier exists with it, and both the cost-down and --- given recoverable
error --- the accuracy-up follow. Where it does not (open physical fact,
free-text truth, real-world physics), the checkable object is the parse,
the proof is post-hoc, and the guarantee is provenance rather than
correctness. This is less a defect of the cascade than its operating
envelope: the results of this paper sit inside it, and this appendix marks
its edge.

\section{Recursive Targets and the Pure-Self-Loop Guard}
\label{sec:recursive-boundary}

This appendix resolves the registered scope limitation of the structural
junk rejection (Section~\ref{sec:threshold-gate}): forbidding the mining
target from length-2 rule bodies removes the self-referential junk rule
without gold labels, but forecloses genuinely recursive targets
(\emph{ancestor}~$\leftarrow$~\emph{ancestor.parent}), so the flag stays
opt-in and licenses ``safe on non-recursive targets'' only. Three locked,
deterministic, fully replayable results at zero provider cost replace that
limitation with a strictly better mechanism: a guard that bans \emph{only}
the pure all-target body (both inversions of
\emph{target}~$\times$~\emph{target}) while keeping mixed
\emph{target}~$\times$~\emph{base} legs.

\paragraph{Result 1: the blunt ban measurably forecloses recursion (synthetic).}
On a seeded random DAG ($n{=}150$, edge probability $0.03$) whose target is
the genuine transitive closure over a base relation, with oracle teachers
and a $3$-seed $\times$ $3$-threshold ($\gamma \in \{0.50, 0.70, 0.90\}$)
$\times$ $3$-arm grid (published / blunt ban / refined guard), the blunt
arm loses forward-chaining coverage of the full closure on \textbf{9/9
recursive cells} (e.g.\ $0.7065 \rightarrow 0.5038$), while the refined arm
preserves published-arm coverage on \textbf{9/9} cells and drops exactly
the pure self-loop installs, nothing else. Unsound derivations are $0$ in
every cell: on a genuinely transitive target even the pure self-loop is
sound --- a subset of the closure --- hence redundant rather than harmful,
which is why coverage preservation and junk removal can coexist. Artifact:
\texttt{experiments/results/e18\_recursive\_target\_synthetic.json}.

\paragraph{Result 2: the refined guard subsumes the published junk rejection on the real grid (classification).}
Re-classifying every \texttt{synthesised\_rules} entry of the committed
MetaQA 2-hop artifact ($22$ cells, both arms), the control arm's
target-containing rules classify \textbf{14/14} as pure self-loops, spread
over exactly the $7$ published junk cells; the true compositions are kept
in \textbf{10/10} cells, every composition rule base-only; and the totals
reconcile with the published aggregates exactly, per cell as well as in
aggregate. Locked decision: \textbf{REFINED-GUARD-SUBSUMES} --- the
refined guard keeps the published junk-rejection behaviour on the
non-recursive grid exactly; combined with Result 1, it is
coverage-preserving on genuinely recursive targets, while the blunt
\texttt{--forbid-target-in-body} flag stays opt-in and non-recursive-only.
Artifact: \texttt{experiments/results/e16\_metaqa\_reread\_refined\_guard.json},
contract-tested in \texttt{tests/test\_e16\_replay\_refined.py}.

\paragraph{Result 3: generative re-mining on the real grid.}
Re-mining all $22$ recorded cells at $\gamma{=}0.50$ under the refined
guard --- teacher answers replayed from the archived recordings, zero
provider calls --- the control arm reproduces the published shape exactly
under the refined taxonomy ($14$ pure-self-loop rules over exactly the $7$
published junk cells; true installs $10/10$), and the refined arm shows
\textbf{zero} pure self-loop and \textbf{zero} mixed-recursive rules across
all $22$ cells, with true compositions kept in \textbf{10/10} at identical
per-rule world precision, no valid cell losing accuracy, and $0$ invalid
cells. Locked decision: \textbf{GENERATIVE-SUBSUMES} --- the junk removal
is not a classification artifact of committed rule lists; it holds when
the miner runs fresh under the guard. Artifact:
\texttt{experiments/results/e18\_w2\_generative\_refined.json}; runner
plumbing and contract test shipped in the same commit.

\paragraph{Explicit non-claims.}
(1) Near-functional leakage (Section~\ref{sec:rule_precision}) is
untouched; the admission-mechanism question remains closed-inconclusive
and is not cited as support. (2) Scope is miner-level: a synthetic
transitive-closure grid with oracle teachers, a classification replay, and
a generative replay of the MetaQA 2-hop grid --- no deployment, no
product, no benchmark beyond MetaQA 2-hop. (3) The archived teacher
recordings stay in the authoring archive; the public artifacts carry
per-cell outcomes only, never raw paid answers.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All three results are deterministic and replayable at zero provider cost
from the seeds and the committed artifacts:
\texttt{experiments/run\_e18\_recursive.py},
\texttt{experiments/replay\_e16\_refined.py}, and
\texttt{experiments/run\_real\_kg\_controlled.py} (replay mode), with
contract tests \texttt{tests/test\_e18\_recursive.py},
\texttt{tests/test\_e16\_replay\_refined.py}, and
\texttt{tests/test\_controlled\_selfloop\_plumbing.py}.
